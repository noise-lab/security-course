| Slide | Context + Links |
|---|---|
| **Why AI Privacy Is Different** | Open with a show of hands: who used an LLM assistant this week? Who shared something personal — homework, a health question, a work email? Most hands stay up. The frame for today is the paradox: we're more privacy-careful than ever, and we overshare to chatbots anyway. Foundational reading: **Zhang et al., "'It's a Fair Game', or Is It?" CHI 2024** — [arXiv:2309.11653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11653) · [DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642385](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642385). 19-user interview + ShareGPT analysis; the paper's thesis (informed consent is defeated by mental models + dark patterns + memorization) is the analytical spine of this lecture. |
| **A 2026 Vignette: Your Chat Logs in Court** | Freshest hook. **Preservation order (May 13, 2025):** Magistrate Judge Ona Wang directed OpenAI to preserve all output logs — [OpenAI's public response](https://openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands/). **Production order (Nov 7, 2025):** Wang ordered 20M de-identified logs from Dec 2022 – Nov 2024, up to 80M prompt-output pairs — [Bloomberg Law summary](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/openai-must-turn-over-20-million-chatgpt-logs-affirms), [Nat'l Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-copyright). **District affirmance (Jan 5, 2026):** Judge Stein affirmed Wang's order. Stein distinguished a wiretap precedent OpenAI cited — ChatGPT users "voluntarily submitted" their conversations, so the privacy interest is weaker. **Sanctions motion (July 9, 2026):** news plaintiffs alleged OpenAI concealed internal searches of its training corpus ("Project Giraffe" Bloom filter; 78M-conversation internal dataset) — [TechCrunch coverage](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/new-york-times-says-openai-hid-evidence-in-chatgpt-copyright-trial/). Teaching point: the privacy properties of a system are not set by its UI — they can be overridden by litigation you never see. Ask: did any of you assume "delete chat" actually deleted it? |
| **Two Kinds of Privacy Risk** | The columns are the framing of Zhang et al. §2 (traditional cloud risks vs. LLM-specific risks). Load-bearing point: the two *compound* — the conversational interface draws out more data, which then flows into all the traditional risks (breach, subpoena, human review). |
| **What Do People Actually Share?** | Categories drawn from ShareGPT analysis in [Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11653) §4.1. Make it concrete: a **job title + employer + city** is often enough to re-identify with no name attached. The conversational format encourages dumping *combinations*. For the ShareGPT provenance itself (public dataset of 90k+ shared ChatGPT convos, later scrubbed for PII issues), the widely-cited Riley Goodside post noting inadvertent PII: <https://twitter.com/goodside/status/1598253337400717313>. |
| **The Interdependent Privacy Problem** | Zhang et al. §5.3 "Interdependent Privacy." Discussion prompt: if a coworker pasted **your** email into ChatGPT to draft a reply to you, how would you feel? Did they have the right? Norms for third-party data in prompts barely exist. Connects forward to the "who owns the data" question that recurs in the copyright lecture. |
| **AI-Specific Risk 1: Memorization** | Canonical result: **Carlini et al., "Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models"** (USENIX Security 2021) — [arXiv:2012.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07805). Follow-up demonstration on production ChatGPT (2023): ["Not Just Memorization"](https://not-just-memorization.github.io/extracting-training-data-from-chatgpt.html) (Carlini et al.), including real names, addresses, phone numbers via a repeated-word prompt. Emphasize: once a fact is baked into weights, removing it is hard — this collides with GDPR's right to erasure. Use only sanitized/public examples if demoing. |
| **AI-Specific Risk 2: The Interface Itself** | Zhang et al. §4.2 on human-like interaction and progressive disclosure. Quote is participant P8, [Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11653). The therapy use case is the sharpest example: highly sensitive mental-health data going to a system with no HIPAA protection. For context on chatbot-as-therapist harms: [Koko controversy (Vice, Jan 2023)](https://www.vice.com/en/article/mental-health-app-koko-chatgpt-experiment). |
| **AI-Specific Risk 3: Implicit Inference** | **Wang, Peddinti, Taft & Feamster, "Beyond PII: How Users Attempt to Estimate and Mitigate Implicit LLM Inference"** — [arXiv:2509.12152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12152) (Sept 15, 2025). Method: survey of 240 U.S. participants, judged inference risks in text snippets, attempted rewrites, compared against ChatGPT and Rescriber. Key numbers: participants performed only **slightly better than chance** at anticipating what could be inferred; **user rewrites blocked inference in just 28%** of cases (better than the Rescriber tool, worse than ChatGPT itself); **paraphrasing** was most popular and *least* effective; **abstraction / added ambiguity** worked better. Reframes the whole lecture: the threat isn't only what you type, it's what the model deduces from context (age, gender, location, income, ethnicity, health). Sanitization tools mostly fail. |
| **How Do Users *Think* LLMs Work?** | Mental models from Zhang et al. §5.1 (n=19 interviews). Model counts in the paper: **Model A ("magic"):** 4 participants. **Model B ("super searcher"):** 8. **Model C ("stochastic parrot"):** 6. **Model D (input = quality signal):** ~majority — misses memorization. **Model E (input = training data):** ~5 correct understanding. Quick activity: have students sketch their own model in 60 seconds, then poll. The punchline lands harder when they realize their model is one of the "wrong" ones. Flawed mental models are the core problem for informed consent. |
| **The Opt-Out Problem and Dark Patterns** | Zhang et al. §5.2: **14 of 19** participants were unaware conversations are used for training by default; all but P11/P14/P16/P17/P18 didn't know an opt-out existed. Dark-pattern taxonomy: [deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/) (Brignull's catalog — obstruction, interface interference, misdirection, bundling). Bundling is the load-bearing one here: turning off training also kills chat history. Likely midterm item — show an LLM settings screen and ask students to name the pattern. |
| **How Users Cope With the Trade-off** | Zhang et al. §6 "Strategies." Quote is participant P10. Tension: the more you sanitize, the less useful the tool — and the "Beyond PII" inference result shows sanitization often fails anyway (Strategy 3b connects directly to that paper). |
| **Is It Really a "Fair Game"?** | This is the analytical thesis of [Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11653) — the paper title's rhetorical question. Informed consent requires comprehension; the system design defeats comprehension. Echo the respect-for-persons / autonomy framing from Lecture 1. |
| **What Should Change: Design** | Design mitigations from Zhang et al. §7 (transparency, unbundled controls) + Wang et al. "Beyond PII" §6 (inference-aware tools: alerts when inputs enable profiling, suggested lower-inference rephrasings, disclosure of what the model can infer). Frame: shift from privacy-by-configuration (user must know settings) to privacy-by-design (default protective). |
| **What Should Change: Policy** | **GDPR right-to-erasure vs. weights:** overview at [EDPB "AI Privacy Risks & Mitigations in LLMs" (Apr 2025)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-04/ai-privacy-risks-and-mitigations-in-llms.pdf). **EU AI Act — GPAI obligations:** entered into application **2 Aug 2025** (models on market before that date have until **2 Aug 2027** to comply; enforcement powers vest **2 Aug 2026**) — [European Commission GPAI Guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers) · [implementation timeline](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/) · [Baker McKenzie summary](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2025/08/general-purpose-ai-obligations). **FTC AI guidance:** ["Keep your AI claims in check" (Feb 2023)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2023/02/keep-your-ai-claims-check). Structural mismatch: privacy law assumes a database you can query and delete; LLMs encode it in weights. |
| **Discussion / Activity** | Think–Pair–Share (10 min). Timer per phase, circulate during pairs, take 3–4 shares, synthesize. Likely themes: financial data, work/school policy, medical info, fear of being "found out" using AI. For the debate seed ("providers must obtain explicit opt-in before training"), route to the [accountability debate](../../debates/accountability.md). Hands-on LLM lab: [`activities/llm.md`](../../activities/llm.md). Breakout companion: [`breakouts/ai-privacy.md`](../../breakouts/ai-privacy.md). |
| **Key Takeaways** | Land the asymmetry thesis: users *believe* it's a fair game because they can't see the risks. Bridge to next lecture: the same training data that creates memorization risk is at the center of the copyright/fair-use fight — Bartz v. Anthropic and Kadrey v. Meta (both June 2025). |

## Additional context

**Case-study candidates for opening hook (in order of freshness):**

1. **NYT v. OpenAI chat-log production** (used in slide 2 vignette; freshest). Preferred default.
2. **Samsung ChatGPT ban (May 2023)** — three separate leaks of source code, meeting notes, and equipment data → company-wide ban. Still useful for the "policy-vs-productivity" tension but no longer freshest; keep as backup. [Bloomberg coverage](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-05-02/samsung-bans-chatgpt-and-other-generative-ai-use-by-staff-after-leak).
3. **Italy ChatGPT ban (March 2023)** — first country to block ChatGPT over GDPR concerns; OpenAI made changes, ban lifted after ~1 month. Anchors the "regulators can move fast" point. [BBC coverage](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-65139406).
4. **March 2023 Redis bug** — 1.2% of ChatGPT Plus users had chat titles and payment info exposed. Anchors the "even well-resourced companies have incidents" point. [OpenAI post-mortem](https://openai.com/blog/march-20-chatgpt-outage).

**Debate topics (route to `../../debates/accountability.md`):**

- *Resolved:* LLM providers must obtain **explicit opt-in** before using user data for training.
- Who owns the burden — **users** protecting themselves, or **design** protecting them?
- Should there be a separate legal category for AI mental-health / companion apps (HIPAA-adjacent regulation)?
