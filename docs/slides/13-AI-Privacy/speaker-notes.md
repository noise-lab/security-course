|  | **Why AI Privacy Is Different** | Open with a show of hands: who used an LLM assistant this week? Who shared something personal — homework, a health question, a work email? Most hands stay up. The frame for today is the paradox: we're more privacy-careful than ever, and we overshare to chatbots anyway. Foundational reading: **Zhang et al., "'It's a Fair Game', or Is It?" CHI 2024** — [arXiv:2309.11653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11653) · [DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642385](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642385) · course copy `docs/readings/llm-disclosure.pdf`. 19-user interview + ShareGPT analysis; the paper's thesis (informed consent is defeated by mental models + dark patterns + memorization) is the analytical spine of this lecture. |
|  | **A 2026 Vignette: Your Chat Logs in Court** | Freshest hook. **Preservation order (May 13, 2025):** Magistrate Judge Ona Wang directed OpenAI to preserve all output logs — [OpenAI's public response](https://openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands/). **Production order (Nov 7, 2025):** Wang ordered 20M de-identified logs from Dec 2022 – Nov 2024 — [Bloomberg Law summary](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/openai-must-turn-over-20-million-chatgpt-logs-affirms), [Nat'l Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-copyright). **District affirmance (Jan 5, 2026):** Judge Stein affirmed in full (In re OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation, SDNY MDL, No. 1:23-cv-11195); privacy interests held adequately protected by the sampling, de-identification, and protective order; wiretap cases distinguished because users "voluntarily submitted" their communications. **Sanctions motion (July 9, 2026):** news plaintiffs alleged OpenAI concealed internal searches of its training corpus ("Project Giraffe" Bloom filter; 78M-conversation internal dataset) — [TechCrunch coverage](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/new-york-times-says-openai-hid-evidence-in-chatgpt-copyright-trial/); OpenAI's opposition filed Aug 2026, no ruling as of Nov 2026. Teaching point: the privacy properties of a system are not set by its UI — they can be overridden by litigation you never see. Ask: did any of you assume "delete chat" actually deleted it? |
|  | **Two Kinds of Privacy Risk** | *Figure:* Graphviz, two clustered rows — AI-specific (memorization, human-like interaction, implicit inference, prompt injection) above traditional (breach, unauthorized access, sale, human review, legal holds) — with one red edge from the interface to the traditional row labelled "draws out more data." The columns are the framing of Zhang et al. §2; prompt injection is the deck's addition for agentic assistants (own slide below). Load-bearing point: the two *compound* — the conversational interface draws out more data, which then flows into all the traditional risks (breach, subpoena, human review). |
|  | **What Do People Actually Share?** | *Figure:* live capture of the [ShareGPT52K dataset card on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/RyokoAI/ShareGPT52K) (captured Nov 18, 2026): the corpus Zhang et al. sampled, PII detected with Microsoft Presidio; the preview rows are real shared conversations. The consented successor is [WildChat-1M](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/WildChat-1M) (AI2). Categories from [Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11653) §3.2.1. Make it concrete: a **job title + employer + city** is often enough to re-identify with no name attached. The conversational format encourages dumping *combinations*. ShareGPT provenance: the Riley Goodside post flagging inadvertent PII, <https://twitter.com/goodside/status/1598253337400717313>. |
|  | **Where Does a Prompt Go?** | *Figure:* Graphviz data flow — prompt → provider storage (retention 30 days to 5 years to indefinite) → training (default on in most consumer products), human review, memory/profile; dashed: litigation hold, vendors, breach; red: fragments extractable from weights. This is the map for Step 2 of the [hands-on](../../activities/llm.md): every arrow is one of its five questions. Retention anchors: 30 days for Anthropic consumer users who decline training and for API traffic; five years for consumer users who allow training ([Anthropic, Aug 28, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updates-to-our-consumer-terms)); ChatGPT history is kept until deleted ([OpenAI Data Controls FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7730893-data-controls-faq), the page the paper's footnote 7 cites; it blocks headless capture, so it is a link). Cold-call: which arrow could you turn off today without losing a feature you want? |
|  | **The Interdependent Privacy Problem** | *Figure:* Graphviz — colleague, clients, students, family on the left, each with what gets pasted (their email, a spreadsheet of client records, student work, personal details), converging on "You (the only one who consented)" and then one red edge to the provider. Zhang et al. §3.2.2 and §6.1.2 "Interdependent Privacy": the use cases are new (drafting a reply to a colleague's email means pasting the colleague's email, which no search engine asked you to do). Discussion prompt: if a coworker pasted **your** email into ChatGPT to draft a reply to you, how would you feel? Did they have the right? Norms for third-party data in prompts barely exist; FERPA and HIPAA cover slivers, nothing covers coworkers, friends, family. Connects forward to the "who owns the data" question in the copyright lecture. |
|  | **AI-Specific Risk 1: Memorization** | *Figure:* Graphviz chain — you type sensitive data → provider stores it → it feeds training → the model memorizes a fragment → another user's prompt extracts it (replaces the old ASCII block). Canonical result: **Carlini et al., "Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models"** (USENIX Security 2021) — [arXiv:2012.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07805). Emphasize: once a fact is baked into weights, removing it is hard — this collides with GDPR's right to erasure; the [EDPB "AI Privacy Risks & Mitigations in LLMs" (Apr 2025)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-04/ai-privacy-risks-and-mitigations-in-llms.pdf) wrestles with exactly this. Use only sanitized/public examples if demoing. |
|  | **Extraction From a Production Model** | *Figure:* live capture of ["Extracting Training Data from ChatGPT"](https://not-just-memorization.github.io/extracting-training-data-from-chatgpt.html) (Nasr, Carlini et al., Nov 28, 2023; captured Nov 18, 2026). **Read first:** the post itself, five minutes. **What happened:** "repeat the word 'poem' forever" makes the model diverge and emit verbatim training data; ~$200 of queries recovered several MB, with a gigabyte estimated reachable; output included real names, phone numbers, email and street addresses. **What to say:** alignment hid the memorization rather than removing it; OpenAI patched the specific prompt within days, a symptom fix. **Course tie-in:** the hands-on's Step 1 case study ("memorization or inference?") — this one is memorization. |
|  | **AI-Specific Risk 2: The Interface Itself** | *Figure:* Graphviz loop — you share a little → warm, human-sounding reply → trust rises → you share more → "can you tell me more?" → repeat. Zhang et al. §3.2.3 and §6.1.4 on human-like interaction and progressive disclosure; the paper observed the model itself driving the loop in a therapist role-play. Quote is participant P8. **Exam framing (anthropomorphization):** people extend social trust to an agent that uses language like a person; trust lowers the guard that a form or search box keeps up; so data comes out one turn at a time. The therapy use case is the sharpest example: mental-health data with no HIPAA protection. Context: [Koko controversy (Vice, Jan 2023)](https://www.vice.com/en/article/mental-health-app-koko-chatgpt-experiment); the FTC's Sept 2025 companion-chatbot inquiry (enforcement slide) is the regulator noticing this loop in products aimed at teenagers. |
|  | **AI-Specific Risk 3: Implicit Inference** | **Wang, Peddinti, Taft & Feamster, "Beyond PII: How Users Attempt to Estimate and Mitigate Implicit LLM Inference"** — [arXiv:2509.12152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12152) (Sept 15, 2025). Method: survey of 240 U.S. participants, judged inference risks in text snippets, attempted rewrites, compared against ChatGPT and Rescriber. Key numbers: participants performed only **slightly better than chance** at anticipating what could be inferred; **user rewrites blocked inference in just 28%** of cases; **paraphrasing** was most popular and *least* effective; **abstraction / added ambiguity** worked better. Reframes the lecture: the threat isn't only what you type, it's what the model deduces from context. |
|  | **AI-Specific Risk 4: Agents and Prompt Injection** | *Figure:* Graphviz — "summarize this page" + a page carrying hidden instructions → agent with your files, inbox, memory, and tools → follows them → exfiltration to the attacker. **Read first:** [OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, LLM01: Prompt Injection](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/) — defines direct (the user's own prompt) vs **indirect** injection (instructions hidden in a web page or file the model reads), and lists the impacts: disclosing sensitive information, unauthorized use of functions available to the model, executing commands in connected systems; its example scenario is an injected image URL that leaks the private conversation. **What to say:** the model has one input channel and cannot reliably tell data from instructions; the privacy harm scales with what the agent *holds* (memory, inbox, files, logins), and persistent memory lets an injected instruction survive across sessions; defenses are mitigations (least-privilege tools, confirmation before outbound actions, isolating untrusted content), not a boundary. **Exam framing:** AI-specific, not traditional — it exists only because the system is a language model. Cold-call: which AI-specific risks does an on-device model fix? Not this one, if the local agent has tools. |
|  | **How Do Users *Think* LLMs Work?** | Mental models from Zhang et al. §5.1 (n=19 interviews). Model counts in the paper: **Model A ("magic"):** 4 participants. **Model B ("super searcher"):** 8. **Model C ("stochastic parrot"):** 6. **Model D (input = quality signal):** ~majority — misses memorization. **Model E (input = training data):** ~5 correct understanding. Quick activity: have students sketch their own model in 60 seconds, then poll. The punchline lands harder when they realize their model is one of the "wrong" ones. Flawed mental models are the core problem for informed consent. |
|  | **The Opt-Out Problem and Dark Patterns** | Zhang et al. §5.6: **14 of 19** participants were unaware conversations are used for training by default; all but P11/P14/P16/P17/P18 didn't know an opt-out existed; the discoverable opt-out forced chat history off at the same time, and the account-level one was a separate FAQ form. Dark-pattern taxonomy: [deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/) (Brignull — obstruction, interface interference, misdirection, bundling). **Exam framing ("remember our chats?" prompt):** not deception, since memory is a real feature; a dark pattern to the extent it names the benefit and omits the cost (retention, training, subpoena), bundles memory with training, and defaults on with a harder decline path. Show an LLM settings screen and ask students to name the pattern. |
|  | **Memory and the Default Flip (2025)** | *Figure:* live capture of [Anthropic, "Updates to Consumer Terms and Privacy Policy," Aug 28, 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updates-to-our-consumer-terms) (captured Nov 18, 2026). **Read first:** that post (five minutes), then [TechCrunch, Aug 28, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/28/anthropic-users-face-a-new-choice-opt-out-or-share-your-data-for-ai-training). **What happened:** Free/Pro/Max chats and Claude Code sessions train new models unless the user declines; retention becomes **five years** for those who allow it vs the existing 30 days; existing users had to choose by **Oct 8, 2025** (first announced as Sept 28) to keep using Claude; Work, Gov, Education, and API traffic excluded. TechCrunch/The Verge described the dialog: large "Accept" button, small training toggle below it defaulted to **On**. **What to say:** Anthropic calls it opt-in (a choice is required); the press called it opt-out (the default is on); both describe the same screen, and the dark-pattern vocabulary (default effect, visual hierarchy, bundling with a terms acceptance) says precisely what is wrong without calling it fraud. **Course tie-in:** the carve-out tracks willingness to pay, like the NYT litigation hold (Enterprise/Edu exempt) — two-tier privacy. Memory features (ChatGPT, Claude) are a separate retention/consent question from per-conversation training; the hands-on asks students to find the switch. Cold-call: redesign the pop-up so it is not a dark pattern but still gets a meaningful opt-in rate. |
|  | **How Users Cope With the Trade-off** | *Figure:* Graphviz — a task that needs personal data → (1) accept ("it's a fair game"), (2) avoid (no real financial numbers, no real symptoms), (3) sanitize (fake names, redact the pasted email, generalities) with (3b) rewrite to strip identifiers dashed off it (28%), (4) follow company policy / NDA. Zhang et al. §5.3 strategies plus the policy pattern from §5.2: P15 "there is a price… it's a fair game"; P19 wanted financial advice but would not type household income and number of kids, got generic advice, gave up; P3 named a different campus, P13 gave fake names; P4/P18 stripped personal details from emails and added them back after; P17 pasted a few sentences of his paper at a time; P5/P10/P11/P14/P15 refused work data because of company policy or NDA (one forgot the rule mid-task). Quote is participant P10. Tension: the more you sanitize, the less useful the tool — and "Beyond PII" shows sanitization often fails anyway. |
|  | **Is It Really a "Fair Game"?** | This is the analytical thesis of [Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11653) — the paper title's rhetorical question. Informed consent requires comprehension; the system design defeats comprehension. Echo the respect-for-persons / autonomy framing from Lecture 1. |
|  | **What Should Change: Design** | Design mitigations from Zhang et al. §6.2 (transparency, unbundled controls, no dark patterns, local models for specific domains) + Wang et al. "Beyond PII" §6 (inference-aware tools: alerts when inputs enable profiling, suggested lower-inference rephrasings, disclosure of what the model can infer). Differential privacy and federation reduce memorization at a real utility cost, which is why they are not everywhere. Frame: shift from privacy-by-configuration (user must know settings) to privacy-by-design (default protective). |
|  | **What Should Change: Policy** | **GDPR right-to-erasure vs. weights:** [EDPB "AI Privacy Risks & Mitigations in LLMs" (Apr 2025)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2025-04/ai-privacy-risks-and-mitigations-in-llms.pdf). **EU AI Act — GPAI obligations:** entered into application **2 Aug 2025** (models on market before that date have until **2 Aug 2027**; Commission enforcement powers from **2 Aug 2026**) — [European Commission GPAI Guidelines](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers) · [implementation timeline](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/). **FTC:** ["Keep your AI claims in check" (Feb 2023)](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2023/02/keep-your-ai-claims-check) and the Sept 2025 companion-chatbot 6(b) inquiry (next slide). Structural mismatch: privacy law assumes a database you can query and delete; LLMs encode it in weights; inference and injection are risks no statute names. |
|  | **Enforcement Is Catching Up (2024–2026)** | *Figure:* Graphviz timeline, two rows of three. **Dec 20, 2024 — Garante fines OpenAI €15M** (decision of Nov 2, 2024: no legal basis, transparency failures, no age verification, unreported March 2023 breach; six-month information campaign ordered) — [DataGuidance summary](https://www.dataguidance.com/news/italy-garante-fines-openai-eu15m-gdpr-non-compliance). **May 19, 2025 — Garante fines Luka Inc. (Replika) €5M** (decision of Apr 10, 2025: no legal basis, inadequate notice, no age gate; second probe opened into model training) — [Privacy Daily](https://privacy-daily.com/article/2025/05/19/garante-fines-us-company-56-million-for-chatbot-gdpr-breaches-2505190007). **Aug 2, 2025 — AI Act GPAI duties apply.** **Sep 11, 2025 — FTC 6(b) orders** to Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap, xAI (3-0 vote): how companion chatbots handle conversation data, monetize engagement, and protect children and teens; a study, not an enforcement action — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions) (blocks headless capture; link only). **Mar 18, 2026 — Court of Rome annuls the €15M fine** (judgment 4153/2026, R.G. 4785/2025): OpenAI Ireland established Feb 2024, so the Irish DPC became lead authority under the GDPR one-stop-shop and the Garante lacked competence; the court did not reach the merits; Garante may appeal — [case summary](https://gdprfine.com/cases/openai-15-million-garante-2024). **Aug 2, 2026 — Commission enforcement powers over GPAI vest.** Teaching point: a model is global, the regulator is national, and the company's choice of EU establishment decides who can fine it. Cold-call: loophole, or the only workable design for a single market? |
