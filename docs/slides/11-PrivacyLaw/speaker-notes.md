| Slide | Context + Links |
|---|---|
| **Privacy Law Always Chases Technology** | Framing quote from Warren & Brandeis, "The Right to Privacy," *Harvard Law Review*, 1890 — primary source PDF: [Cornell mirror](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~shmat/courses/cs5436/warren-brandeis.pdf). Warren was reportedly enraged by Boston newspapers covering his family's social events with then-new instantaneous photography; the article invented the U.S. right-to-privacy tort. Teaching move: name the pattern (new tech → new harm → new law) and hold it as the deck's spine. Ask students the 2026 analogue — model training on scraped data? age-verification wallets? |
| **A 2026 Hook: The FTC Still Sets the Floor** | **Disney COPPA $10M** — [FTC press release, Dec. 30/31, 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/court-approves-order-requiring-disney-pay-10-million-settle-ftc-allegations-firm-enabled-unlawful); background on the earlier September 2025 filing: [FTC case page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/disney). Disney allegedly failed to label some YouTube uploads "Made for Kids" (the designation Google agreed to in the 2019 YouTube COPPA settlement), enabling targeted ads to under-13s. Order also requires a structured MFK-review compliance program. **Kochava** — [FTC ban on sale of sensitive location data, May 4, 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data); [FTC case docket](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/ftc-v-kochava-inc). Nearly 4 years of litigation; the case rests on the *unfairness* prong of Section 5 (no broken promise required) — the more legally interesting theory. Both anchors make the same teaching point: no federal statute → a 1998 kids' law and a 1914 consumer-protection statute do the work. |
| **Computerized Records Created New Threats** | Standard historical anchor — the four 1970s threats (unknown DBs, mass collection, dissemination, linkage) are all worse today. If a student pushes back, note that record-linkage attacks (Sweeney's next slide) empirically confirm the 1970s intuition was right. |
| **FIPPs: The Foundation (HEW, 1973)** | Primary source: HEW, *Records, Computers, and the Rights of Citizens* (1973) — [full report at ASPE/HHS](https://aspe.hhs.gov/reports/records-computers-rights-citizens). Every modern regime (OECD 1980 guidelines, EU 1995 Directive, GDPR, CCPA) descends from these five principles. Have students memorize them — they map cleanly onto GDPR Articles 5, 12–22. |
| **The FIPPs in Practice** | The opt-in / opt-out slide. Canonical empirical result: default-opt-in is ~90%+ compliance; default-opt-out is ~<5%. See [Johnson & Goldstein, "Do Defaults Save Lives?" *Science* 302:1338 (2003)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1091721). The one design choice controls everything downstream — cookie banners, breach-notification opt-outs, GDPR consent, CCPA opt-out. Foreshadows Meeting 12's dark-patterns study. |
| **Notice and Consent Is Broken** | Live demo: [ToS;DR](https://tosdr.org/). Canonical reading: [Aleecia McDonald & Lorrie Cranor, "The Cost of Reading Privacy Policies," *I/S: A Journal of Law and Policy* (2008)](https://kb.osu.edu/handle/1811/72839) — 244 hours/year per person to read the policies of the sites they visit. Solove's [*"Privacy Self-Management and the Consent Dilemma"*, 126 Harv. L. Rev. 1880 (2013)](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-126/introduction-privacy-self-management-and-the-consent-dilemma/) is the canonical critique of putting the burden on the individual. |
| **Re-Examining FIPPs in the Age of Inference** | The [Target/pregnancy-prediction story](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kashmirhill/2012/02/16/how-target-figured-out-a-teen-girl-was-pregnant-before-her-father-did/) (Kashmir Hill, *Forbes*, Feb. 2012) is the canonical example — no PII leaked, deeply private fact produced. On differential privacy: [Dwork & Roth, *The Algorithmic Foundations of Differential Privacy* (2014)](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~aaroth/Papers/privacybook.pdf) — Ch. 1 is the readable intro to why noise *is* the privacy. The paradox: some FIPPs (accuracy, minimization-of-error) point the opposite direction from what actually protects privacy. |
| **PII Is Not a Bright Line** | **AOL 2006:** [Barbaro & Zeller, "A Face Is Exposed for AOL Searcher No. 4417749," *NYT*, Aug. 9, 2006](https://www.nytimes.com/2006/08/09/technology/09aol.html). **Netflix Prize 2006:** [Narayanan & Shmatikov, "Robust De-anonymization of Large Sparse Datasets"](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~shmat/shmat_oak08netflix.pdf) (S&P 2008). **Sweeney/MA governor:** [Sweeney, "Simple Demographics Often Identify People Uniquely" (2000)](https://dataprivacylab.org/projects/identifiability/paper1.pdf) — 87% of US residents uniquely identified by ZIP + DOB + sex. Modern update: [Rocher, Hendrickx & de Montjoye, "Estimating the success of re-identifications in incomplete datasets using generative models," *Nature Communications* (2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10933-3) — 15 attributes suffice for >99.98% of Americans. |
| **Sectoral vs. Omnibus** | Burn this dichotomy in — midterm staple. Best-of-both defenders point to Illinois BIPA (a sectoral state law with a private right of action → $650M Facebook settlement) as evidence that sectoral can be sharp when it wants to be. |
| **The Patchwork, Statute by Statute** | Bork VPPA story is the keeper — see [Michael Dolan, "The Bork Tapes," *City Paper*, Sept. 25, 1987](https://www.washingtoncitypaper.com/article/173390/the-bork-tapes-saga/); Congress passed the [VPPA, 18 U.S.C. § 2710](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2710), in Oct. 1988, ~a year later. The FCRA/FERPA/HIPAA/GLBA/COPPA/GINA statute list is the canonical "sectoral by anecdote" survey — dates in the deck are correct (double-check GINA is 2008). |
| **The HIPAA Gap** | Primary FTC releases: [GoodRx $1.5M (Feb 1, 2023)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/02/ftc-enforcement-action-bar-goodrx-sharing-consumers-sensitive-health-info-advertising); [BetterHelp $7.8M (Mar 2, 2023)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/03/ftc-ban-betterhelp-revealing-consumers-data-including-sensitive-mental-health-information-facebook). Key teaching move: HIPAA regulates *covered entities* (providers, plans, clearinghouses, and their BAs) — not "health data as such." Consumer apps, wearables, period trackers, 23andMe, telehealth-adjacent services fall outside. FTC Section 5 has to reach into that gap. |
| **Breach Notification: Regulation by Consequence** | California SB 1386 (2003) is the origin — [California Civil Code §§1798.29, 1798.82](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.82.). State law map: [NCSL breach-notification tracker](https://www.ncsl.org/technology-and-communication/security-breach-notification-laws) — all 50 states + DC, PR, VI now have laws. The teaching frame: notification didn't mandate security; it made *insecurity* a headline. |
| **What a Breach Law Requires Varies** | The "strictest-state-wins" dynamic is why California's law effectively becomes the national floor — same "California effect" you'll see in Meeting 12's spillover finding. |
| **Equifax (2017): What a Bad Response Looks Like** | Official record: [FTC Equifax settlement page (~$700M, July 2019)](https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds/equifax-data-breach-settlement). Contemporaneous coverage of the spoofable notification site: [Krebs, "Equifax Breach Response Turns Dumpster Fire," Sept. 2017](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2017/09/equifax-breach-response-turns-dumpster-fire/). Data subjects were never Equifax customers — the definition of "harm not reasonably avoidable" for FTC unfairness. |
| **Section 5 of the FTC Act** | Statute text: [15 U.S.C. § 45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45). The FTC's own explainer: [A Brief Overview of the FTC's Consumer Protection Authority](https://www.ftc.gov/about-ftc/mission/enforcement-authority). Deception theory: [FTC 1983 Policy Statement on Deception](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-deception); Unfairness theory: [FTC 1980 Policy Statement on Unfairness](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/ftc-policy-statement-unfairness). Solove & Hartzog's book [*The FTC and the New Common Law of Privacy*](https://scholarship.law.gwu.edu/faculty_publications/622/) (Columbia L. Rev. 2014) is the definitive academic take. |
| **Section 5 in Action** | Primary FTC releases: [Uber (Aug 2017)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2017/08/uber-settles-ftc-allegations-it-made-deceptive-privacy-data-security-claims); [Facebook $5B (July 2019)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/07/ftc-imposes-5-billion-penalty-sweeping-new-privacy-restrictions-facebook); [Twitter 2FA-for-ads $150M (May 2022)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/05/ftc-charges-twitter-deceptively-using-account-security-data-sell-targeted-ads); Disney COPPA — see opening vignette. Facebook's 2019 order set the "20-year comprehensive privacy program + executive certification" template that later orders adopt. Ask: is governance by consent decree a feature (flexible, evolves case by case) or a bug (no democratic input, no published rulemaking)? |
| **GDPR: Scope and Reach** | Statute: [Regulation (EU) 2016/679 — EUR-Lex consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj). Article 3 (territorial scope) is the extraterritoriality hook. The "Brussels effect" thesis: [Anu Bradford, *The Brussels Effect: How the European Union Rules the World* (OUP 2020)](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-brussels-effect-9780190088583). |
| **What GDPR Requires** | Right to erasure (Art. 17): [Google Spain v. AEPD (2014)](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/document/document.jsf?docid=152065&doclang=EN) is the "right to be forgotten" ruling. Cross-border transfers (Chapter V, Arts. 44–50): the [Schrems I (2015)](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/document/document.jsf?docid=169195) and [Schrems II (2020)](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/document/document.jsf?docid=228677) rulings invalidated Safe Harbor and Privacy Shield respectively; the [EU–US Data Privacy Framework (July 2023)](https://www.dataprivacyframework.gov/) is the current successor and already under legal challenge. DPIAs: Art. 35. |
| **GDPR Enforcement Is Real** | Live tracker: [GDPR Enforcement Tracker (CMS)](https://www.enforcementtracker.com/). Cumulative-fine benchmark: [DLA Piper GDPR Fines and Data Breach Survey, Jan. 2026 — €7.1B through Jan. 10, 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/dla-piper-gdpr-and-data-breach-survey-2026). Primary sources for the vignette: **TikTok €530M** — [Irish DPC press release, May 2, 2025](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following) (€485M Art. 46, €45M Art. 13(1)(f); Chinese personnel remote access; the [High Court stayed the transfer-suspension order pending TikTok's appeal in Sept. 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/09/23/tiktok-wins-stay-on-irish-data-transfers-to-china-suspension-order/)). **CNIL Google €325M (Sept. 1, 2025)** — [CNIL press release](https://www.cnil.fr/en/cookies-and-advertisements-inserted-between-emails-google-fined-325-million-euros-cnil): Gmail "Promotions"/"Social" tabs contained ad emails without consent, plus a 6-clicks-to-refuse vs. 2-clicks-to-accept cookie flow when creating a Google account (74M French accounts affected). **Meta €1.2B (2023)** — [Irish DPC/EDPB decision, May 22, 2023](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/data-protection-commission-announces-conclusion-two-inquiries-meta-ireland). |
| **The GDPR Debate** | Natural debate prompt — see [`../../debates/omnibus.md`](../../debates/omnibus.md). Strongest "against" argument: compliance cost creates a moat for incumbents that can afford CPOs and DPIA teams. Strongest "for": the 1995 Data Protection Directive was omnibus on paper but famously unenforced; GDPR is what enforcement actually looks like — €7.1B says so. |
| **Where U.S. Privacy Law Stands in 2026** | Primary trackers: [IAPP US State Privacy Legislation Tracker](https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-privacy-legislation-tracker) (canonical; last updated late June 2026) and [MultiState 2026 map](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/2/4/all-of-the-comprehensive-privacy-laws-that-take-effect-in-2026). **20 comprehensive state laws in effect in 2026**, with Indiana, Kentucky, and Rhode Island effective Jan. 1, 2026; Connecticut/Arkansas/Utah amendments effective July 1, 2026. 2025 was the first year in five with no new comprehensive law enacted — but 2026 is bringing a fresh wave (Alabama passed both chambers as of April, awaiting governor). On the federal front: [APRA expired at end of 118th Congress (Jan. 2025) and has not been reintroduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Privacy_Rights_Act) — killed by fights over CA preemption and the private right of action. Only new federal privacy law of 2025 is the [TAKE IT DOWN Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/146) (non-consensual intimate imagery + deepfakes, 48-hour removal, enforcement live May 2026). Texas's [$1.375B Google settlement (Oct 31, 2025)](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-historic-1375-billion-settlement-google-related-texans-data) is the biggest single-state privacy settlement ever — a signal that state AGs, not Congress, are where the enforcement teeth live. Bridge to Meeting 12: [CA AG's $2.75M Disney and $12.75M GM settlements (Feb/May 2026)](../../../slides/12-Compliance/speaker-notes.md) prove enforcement of *functional* opt-out is now real. |
| **Takeaways** | Return to the opening pattern — each of the three current enforcement anchors (Disney COPPA, TikTok €530M, CNIL Google €325M) is a law from a prior decade catching up to a technology that outran it. Next meeting: automated compliance. |
