# Lecture 17 — Speaker Notes (Internet Censorship)

Per-slide context + clickable links. In-deck notes (the `::: {.notes}` blocks) are also visible via reveal.js speaker view (press **S**). Every URL was verified this session or is a canonical org landing page.

| Slide | Context + Links |
|---|---|
| **"The Net Interprets Censorship as Damage"** | Quote is by [John Gilmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gilmore_(activist)), EFF co-founder. The *Time* piece it appears in: [Philip Elmer-DeWitt, "First Nation in Cyberspace," *Time*, Dec 6, 1993](https://time.com/archive/6724389/first-nation-in-cyberspace/). Provenance write-up (earliest independent record is Rheingold's *The Virtual Community*, Sept 1993): [Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2021/07/12/censor/). Ask the room: do you still believe it? The arc of the lecture is that the Internet has drifted *back* toward a handful of walled platforms — which makes control easier, not harder. |
| **What This Lecture Covers** | Roadmap slide. Emphasize the thesis: modern control is a *tax on attention*, not just a blocked page. Preview the friction/flooding framing that will land at the end. |
| **The What and Why of Censorship** | Book anchor: Roberts, [*Censored*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691178868/censored), Ch. 1. Three verbs — access, publish, view — map to three intervention points. Economic protectionism (favor a domestic platform over a foreign one) is increasingly important and hardest to call "censorship." |
| **A Brief History** | Timeline slide. **China 1994**: Internet arrival. **GFW/Golden Shield**: [Great Firewall — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Firewall). **Google 2006/2010**: [Google in China — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_China). **Egypt 2011 BGP withdrawal**: Cowie/Renesys, "Egypt Leaves the Internet"; ~88% of Egyptian networks disconnected. **Great Cannon 2015**: [Citizen Lab report](https://citizenlab.ca/2015/04/chinas-great-cannon/) on the DDoS-via-injection attack on GreatFire/GitHub. **Cloudflare / Daily Stormer 2017**: [Matthew Prince, "Why We Terminated Daily Stormer"](https://blog.cloudflare.com/why-we-terminated-daily-stormer/) — the pivot to private-actor gatekeepers. |
| **A "Modern" Theory of Censorship** | Roberts's information pyramid. Anchor: [Margaret Roberts, *Censored: Distraction and Diversion Inside China's Great Firewall*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691178868/censored) (Princeton, 2018); author page: <https://margaretroberts.net/>. The censor's job is not to erase the base of the pyramid; it is to control the narrow channel by which stories reach the public. |
| **Censorship Is the Norm, Not the Exception** | **Freedom House Freedom on the Net 2025:** <https://freedomhouse.org/report/freedom-net/2025> is the source of the country-count framing. **Vignette (verified this session, 2026-07-16):** Access Now #KeepItOn 2025 annual report (Mar 31, 2026), <https://www.accessnow.org/internet-shutdowns-2025/> and the report PDF at <https://www.accessnow.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/KeepItOn-Internet-Shutdowns-2025-Annual-Report.pdf> — **313 shutdowns / 52 countries / highest since tracking began in 2016**; **Myanmar #1 (95), India #2 (65)** for the second year running; **conflict** is the leading trigger for the third consecutive year (125 shutdowns in 14 countries); **70 shutdowns coinciding with grave human-rights abuses in 21 countries**. Economic-loss estimate ($19.7B) is from [Top10VPN / NetBlocks](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/internet-outage-statistics/) — Russia alone at $11.9B. The **whitelisting pivot** is the key conceptual update: blacklisting is "everything allowed except X," whitelisting is "everything blocked except X." See Russia's [Sovereign Internet Law / RuNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runet) and Iran's [National Information Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Information_Network). |
| **Measuring Censorship** | Tools: [OONI](https://ooni.org/) and [OONI Explorer](https://explorer.ooni.org/) (endpoint measurements from volunteers); [Censored Planet](https://censoredplanet.org/) (remote, continuous, global — the Roya Ensafi / Michigan group); [Google Transparency Report](https://transparencyreport.google.com/); [Access Now KeepItOn](https://www.accessnow.org/campaign/keepiton/) (shutdown tracking); [SFLC.in India shutdown tracker](https://internetshutdowns.in/). Egypt 2011 traffic-collapse plot is the classic pedagogical image. Note: [Herdict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herdict) shut down; the modern equivalents above have replaced it. |
| **The Conventional Picture** | Alice/censor/Bob diagram. The mental model students arrive with — and a fine starting point. The whole rest of the lecture complicates it: the chokepoint moves, and outright blocking is often the *least* favored tool. |
| **Technical Mechanisms** | Walk the stack. **IP filtering** is cheap and coarse. **DNS poisoning** — see the DNS-security lecture and [OONI DNS measurements](https://ooni.org/nettest/dns-consistency/). **TCP RST injection** and **SNI-based filtering** are how the GFW does fine-grained keyword-aware blocking on TLS. **Encrypted DNS (DoH/DoT)** and **Encrypted Client Hello (ECH)** — [Cloudflare on ECH](https://blog.cloudflare.com/encrypted-client-hello) — are eroding the SNI vantage point. Recent measurement: **Hoang et al., *GFWeb* (USENIX Security 2024)** on GFW web-censorship at scale; [USENIX Security 2024 accepted papers](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/technical-sessions). Right column matters more every year: control a **registrar**, a **CA**, or a **CDN** and you control reachability without touching a packet. |
| **Points of Control Along the Path** | Anchor for "protocols to platforms." Preview: control is migrating from the network core toward the platform and the endpoint — mandated client-side scanning (EU Chat Control), app-store removals (Russia VPN apps, 2024), platform bans. |
| **Non-Technical Censorship** | Legal anchors: [DMCA §512](https://www.copyright.gov/dmca/) · [CDA §230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230) · [EU DSA](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/digital-services-act-package). Self-censorship is the hardest to measure and arguably the most powerful. Copyright takedown regimes are the favorite legal tool: fast, deniable, outsourced to platforms. Connects to the moderation and copyright lectures. |
| **Circumvention Tools** | **VPNs**: [Tor Project's overview of censorship circumvention](https://support.torproject.org/censorship/). **Tor + pluggable transports (Snowflake, obfs4, meek)**: <https://tb-manual.torproject.org/circumvention/>. **Signal**: <https://signal.org/blog/> (its domain-fronting and censorship-circumvention work is a good student read). **Domain fronting**: mostly killed when Google/AWS shut it off in 2018; [EFF explainer](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2018/05/domain-fronting-critical-tool-open-web). The arms race motivates the whitelisting pivot in the vignette. |
| **Two Clever Ideas Worth Knowing** | **Decoy / refraction routing**: [Wustrow et al., *Telex: Anticensorship in the Network Infrastructure* (USENIX Security 2011)](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/events/sec11/tech/full_papers/Wustrow.pdf) and follow-ons ([TapDance](https://tapdance.team/), [Conjure](https://refraction.network/)). **Language-based / homophone evasion on Weibo**: [King, Pan & Roberts, "How Censorship in China Allows Government Criticism but Silences Collective Expression" (APSR 2013)](https://gking.harvard.edu/publications/how-censorship-china-allows-government-criticism-silences-collective-expression) and the [50c Party paper (APSR 2017)](https://gking.harvard.edu/50c). Both examples illustrate: censorship is rarely airtight, and the censor often *knows* it isn't. |
| **Trends in Information Control** | Second-half thesis. Anchor essay: [Jack Balkin, "Free Speech in the Algorithmic Society" (UC Davis L. Rev. 2018)](https://lawreview.law.ucdavis.edu/issues/51/3/Symposium/51-3_Balkin.pdf) and Balkin's "free-speech triangle." Also relevant: [Kate Klonick, "The New Governors" (Harvard L. Rev. 2018)](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/vol-131/the-new-governors-the-people-rules-and-processes-governing-online-speech/). Centralization is what makes modern takedown both easy and deniable. |
| **Manipulation: Sock-Puppets, Astroturf, Disinformation** | China's "50 Cent Army": [King, Pan, Roberts, "How the Chinese Government Fabricates Social Media Posts" (APSR 2017)](https://gking.harvard.edu/files/gking/files/how_the_chinese_government_fabricates_social_media_posts_for_strategic_distraction_not_engaged_argument.pdf). Their finding: state commentators mostly *distract* (cheerleading, topic-shifting) rather than argue. Modern twist: generative AI makes plausible sock-puppet content nearly free — see [Goldstein et al., "Generative Language Models and Automated Influence Operations" (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04246). |
| **Roberts' Taxonomy: Fear, Friction, Flooding** | The single most important slide of the deck. Straight from [Roberts, *Censored*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691178868/censored) §1.2 "Distraction and Diversion" (p. 4 onward). Fear targets the sophisticated minority; friction and flooding work on everyone else. The genius is that friction and flooding are *deniable* — they look like a slow network or an organic trend, not a crackdown. Have students classify a fresh headline into one of the three F's as a cold-call. |
| **Censorship as a Tax, Not a Ban** | Direct quote from Roberts (Ch. 1). The **Google 2010** throttling story: rather than a hard block, pages loaded ~75% of the time, slowly. Most users give up; the determined few get through. Deniable — "the network is just slow." This is the "porous censorship" insight: **the holes are a feature**, because they relieve pressure from the motivated minority who would otherwise cause backlash. |
| **Personalization and "Filter Bubbles"** | **Zuckerberg "squirrel dying" quote**: originally in David Kirkpatrick's [*The Facebook Effect*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Facebook_Effect) (Simon & Schuster, 2010). **Eli Pariser filter bubble**: <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filter_bubble>. Empirical pushback worth naming: [Guess et al., "Reshares on social media amplify political news but do not detectably affect beliefs or opinions" (Science 2023)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.add8424) — evidence for filter-bubble *effects* is mixed. The teaching point isn't "filter bubbles are unstoppable" but "whoever controls ranking controls attention." |
| **Why Control Information If It Leaks Anyway?** | This answers the lecture's central puzzle. Perfect censorship was never the goal. A leaky system that taxes attention, looks like technical failure, and avoids martyrs is *more* effective than an airtight one that provokes resistance. The future of censorship is the competition for limited human attention. |
| **Takeaways** | Land the thesis: censorship is **control over the flow** of information, running from **packets to platforms to attention**; the modern toolkit is **fear, friction, flooding**, porous by design; we can **measure** it (OONI, Censored Planet) and **circumvent** it, but the arms race favors whoever controls the chokepoint. |
