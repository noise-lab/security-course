| **From the Four Factors to the Training Set** | Bridge slide. Assumes students know the idea/expression line, the four-factor test, and *Google v. Oracle* from Lecture 14. Statutory backbone: [17 U.S.C. §107 (fair use)](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#107). The organizing question all day: *is copying-to-train fair use, and does it matter how you got the data or what the model outputs?* Per agenda, the for/against AI case-study drill is a near-certain midterm item — make them argue both sides. |
| **A 2026 Vignette: The Law Is Splitting in Real Time** | Four dated, verified rulings. **Thomson Reuters v. Ross** (D. Del., Feb 11, 2025): first federal rejection of AI-training fair use — [Sterne Kessler analysis](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/ai-ip-year-in-review-first-federal-ruling-rejects-fair-use-defense-for-ai-training-data-in-copyright-dispute/) · [Loeb & Loeb summary](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/02/thomson-reuters-v-ross-intelligence-inc). **Bartz v. Anthropic** (N.D. Cal., Jun 23, 2025): training exceedingly transformative but library-piracy not fair use — settlement filings [Susman Godfrey wins page](https://www.susmangodfrey.com/wins/susman-godfrey-secures-1-5-billion-settlement-in-landmark-ai-piracy-case/) · [Copyright Alliance settlement explainer](https://copyrightalliance.org/participating-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement/) · [Authors Guild — what authors need to know](https://authorsguild.org/advocacy/artificial-intelligence/what-authors-need-to-know-about-the-anthropic-settlement/) · [CNBC on Alsup's preliminary approval, Sept 25, 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/25/judge-anthropic-case-preliminary-ok-to-1point5b-settlement-with-authors.html). **Kadrey v. Cal., Jun 25, 2025): Meta win on training + note Chhabria's "market-dilution" dicta — [Goodwin alert](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/alerts-practices-aiml-northern-district-of-california-judge-rules) · [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/kadrey-v-meta-platforms-inc). **NYT v. Third Circuit oral argument on *Ross* was **June 11, 2026** — first appellate word on AI fair use. |
| **How Models "Learn": Copying Is Step Zero** | The legally load-bearing fact: training requires copying the full works into the pipeline. Connect to memorization risk introduced in the AI-Privacy lecture (13). Non-CS framing: traditional programming is a recipe; ML is learning to cook by tasting thousands of dishes. Whether the intermediate copy is fair use is the whole fight. |
| **What Is in the Training Data?** | **Books3** background — ~196k titles scraped from the pirate library Bibliotik; pulled offline in 2023 but persists in trained models. See the [Authors Guild lawsuit page](https://authorsguild.org/news/authors-guild-sues-openai-for-systematic-copyright-infringement/). **Common Crawl** — [commoncrawl.org](https://commoncrawl.org/). **LAION-5B** — [laion.ai](https://laion.ai/). Scale anchor: *Oracle* was 11,500 lines of code; a frontier model trains on a meaningful fraction of the entire public internet. |
| **Does Scale Change the Fair-Use Answer?** | The midterm drill in table form. Have them argue each cell both ways. Factor 1 (purpose) is doing most of the work in the rulings; Factor 4 (market) is the plaintiffs' strongest card and is where Chhabria's *Kadrey* "market-dilution" dicta live. See [Robins Kaplan on *Kadrey*'s cautionary tale](https://www.robinskaplan.com/newsroom/insights/no-harm-no-win-a-cautionary-tale-of-kadrey-v-meta-platforms-inc). |
| **The Lawsuit Landscape** | Table anchors, verified for 2026: **Andersen v. Stability/Midjourney** trial set **Sept 8, 2026** — [NYU JIPEL explainer](https://jipel.law.nyu.edu/andersen-v-stability-ai-the-landmark-case-unpacking-the-copyright-risks-of-ai-image-generators/) · [Knowing Machines legal explainer](https://knowingmachines.org/knowing-legal-machines/legal-explainer/cases/andersen-v-stability-ai). **Getty v. Stability AI (UK)** — copyright claims rejected, narrow trademark win — [High Court judgment PDF (Nov 4, 2025)](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Getty-Images-v-Stability-AI.pdf) · [Mayer Brown alert](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/getty-images-v-stability-ai-what-the-high-courts-decision-means-for-rights-holders-and-ai-developers). Contrast to teach: *Kadrey* and *Bartz* trained overseas or acquired legally → training fair use; *Ross* built a direct competitor → not fair use; Getty (US) still pending and now armed with UK factual findings. |
| **NYT v. OpenAI: The Regurgitation Fight** | NYT is the marquee case because it refused to license while AP, Axel Springer, and the FT signed. Judge Stein narrowed but did not dismiss (Mar 2025). Discovery drama: [Bloomberg Law — 20M ChatGPT logs, Jan 5, 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/openai-must-turn-over-20-million-chatgpt-logs-judge-affirms) · [Nat'l Law Review — privacy gambit lost](https://natlawreview.com/article/openai-loses-privacy-gambit-20-million-chatgpt-logs-likely-headed-copyright). June 2026 amended complaint added a Microsoft "dedicated-supercomputer" theory. Case page: [Wikipedia — NYT v. Microsoft and OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_v._Microsoft_and_OpenAI). Demo idea: ask any current chatbot for a paywalled article and it will refuse — that refusal is post-litigation mitigation, not exoneration. |
| **The Memorization Problem** | Through-line from the AI-Privacy lecture. Canonical extraction work: [Carlini et al., *Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models* (USENIX Security 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07805) · [Carlini et al., *Quantifying Memorization Across Neural Language Models* (ICLR 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07646). Larger models memorize more; duplicated + distinctive text is most extractable. Contrast Google Books (designed never to show full text; snippets only). Open legal question is exactly what *NYT v. OpenAI* is testing. |
| **The "AI Learns Like a Human" Analogy** | Cold-call slide. Steel-man both sides. Strongest pro-analogy point: copyright doesn't protect ideas or learning, only expression → maybe regulate outputs, not training. Strongest anti point: a human who memorizes and re-sells a book is infringing; scale + verbatim memory push AI closer to that case than to a reader. Tie back to the 2025 split: courts increasingly distinguish *how it learned* (often OK) from *what it outputs* (must show substantial similarity). |
| **The Licensing-Market Question** | *Campbell v. Acuff-Rose* line — "copyright holders may not preempt the exploitation of transformative markets" (see [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/)). AI companies use *Campbell*; rights holders counter that the AP/FT/Reddit deals show a real, takeable market. The 2026 doctrinal shift is toward *output* substantial similarity — flag this as the freshest analytic move. Chhabria's *Kadrey* dicta on "market dilution" is the specific hook to name. |
| **Who Owns the *Output*?** | **U.S. Copyright Office, *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence*, Part 2: Copyrightability (Jan 29, 2025)** — human authorship is bedrock; purely AI-generated output not registrable; prompts alone don't confer authorship; human selection/arrangement/modification can be protected case-by-case. Landing page: [copyright.gov/ai](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/). Ties forward to student portfolio work and downstream infringement liability. |
| **International Approaches Diverge** | **Japan** — Art. 30-4 (2018) permits copying for "information analysis" even commercially. **EU** — TDM exception with rights-holder opt-out under [Directive (EU) 2019/790, Art. 4](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/790/oj); the [EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/) adds training-data transparency (a "sufficiently detailed summary"). **UK** — post-*Getty* the [UK Government's full copyright/AI report is due March 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence); a TDM-with-opt-out proposal is on the table but not enacted. **US** — no statute; courts decide case-by-case. Fragmentation creates forum-shopping incentives. |
| **Policy Levers on the Table** | Consent defaults, collective/compulsory licensing (ASCAP/BMI model), watermarking, do-not-train registries, "nutrition labels" for models. **UChicago local hook — creator-side technical self-defense:** [Glaze](https://glaze.cs.uchicago.edu/) and [Nightshade](https://nightshade.cs.uchicago.edu/) from Ben Zhao's lab. Also mention [Spawning's *Have I Been Trained?*](https://haveibeentrained.com/) opt-out database. Every option hits the same four hard problems: valuation, attribution, retroactivity, enforcement. |
| **Activity: Argue the Case Both Ways** | The midterm format. Scenario: 50k novels → chapter-summary subscription service. Expected reads: (1) commercial + arguably non-transformative → against; (2) novels highly creative → against; (3) entire books → against; (4) summaries substitute for buying → against. Likely *not* fair use — useful contrast to *Anthropic*/*Kadrey*, where the training was transformative but a summary-generator is closer to *Ross*'s competing-product problem. | **What Comes Next** | Keep predictions humble. Freshest concrete anchor: **Third Circuit *Ross* oral argument, June 11, 2026** — first appellate ruling on AI fair use is imminent and could set up a circuit split with N.D. Cal. Liability allocation (prompting user vs. model provider) is the next exam-ready frontier; see the [AI Copyright Lawsuits Tracker](https://ailawsuittracker.com/cases/new-york-times-v-openai/) for a live status board. |
| **Key Takeaways** | Recap ladder. Close on the opening idea: the law isn't new, the scale is. The four factors drilled in Lecture 14 are exactly the tools courts are using. For the midterm: be ready to take an AI scenario and argue both fair use and infringement, citing specific factors and the 2025–26 cases by name (Ross, Bartz, Kadrey, NYT). |
