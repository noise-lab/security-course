# Bonus — Speaker Notes (Algorithmic Fairness)

Per-slide context + clickable links. Bonus deck (off-schedule). In-deck notes (`::: {.notes}`) are visible via reveal.js speaker view (press **S**). Terse — enough for a future instructor to pick up.

| Slide | Context + Links |
|---|---|
| **Why Fairness Belongs in This Course** | Frame as the accountability lecture for automated decision-making — pairs with the [AI accountability debate `../../debates/accountability.md`](../../debates/accountability.md). Punchline: "fair" is not a certifiable property; choosing a fairness definition is a value judgment dressed as math. |
| **A 2026 Vignette: AI Hiring on Trial** | ***Mobley v. Workday***: class-certification developments in 2024–2026. Coverage: [Reuters, "Workday must face novel AI bias lawsuit from job applicants" (Jul 2024)](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/workday-must-face-novel-ai-bias-lawsuit-job-applicants-2024-07-15/); the "software vendor as agent of the employer" theory is the load-bearing move. **California ADS regulations** (effective October 2025): [CRD final regulations page](https://calcivilrights.ca.gov/2025/03/21/californias-civil-rights-council-approves-employment-regulations-addressing-automated-decision-systems/). Verify status of the Mobley case before class — it may have progressed. |
| **Overview: What's Settled, What Isn't** | Set expectations: young field, no checklist. Goal is fluency to ask the right questions. |
| **Societal Bias vs. Statistical Bias** | Two distinct leakage points: injustice baked into reality (societal) and distortion in how we sampled reality (statistical). A model can be statistically unbiased and still launder societal injustice. Anchor: [Barocas, Hardt & Narayanan, *Fairness and Machine Learning*](https://fairmlbook.org/) (Ch. 1–2). |
| **Bias in Training Data** | **Recidivism / arrest-vs-crime label leakage**: [Lum & Isaac, "To predict and serve?" (Significance 2016)](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1740-9713.2016.00960.x). **Gendered translation**: [Bolukbasi et al., "Man is to Computer Programmer as Woman is to Homemaker?" (NeurIPS 2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.06520); the Turkish "o bir doktor → he is a doctor" example is now widely documented in LLMs too. Ask: what's the ground-truth label you wish you had, and why can't you measure it? |
| **Minimizing Average Error Fits the Majority** | **Gender Shades**: [Buolamwini & Gebru, "Gender Shades" (FAT* 2018)](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a/buolamwini18a.pdf). Error rates on darker-skinned women dwarfed those on lighter-skinned men across MSFT, Face++, IBM. Mechanism: average loss dominated by the majority. Fix often lives in the dataset, not the loss function. |
| **COMPAS: Predicting Re-Offense** | Anchor: [Angwin et al., "Machine Bias" (ProPublica, May 2016)](https://www.propublica.org/article/machine-bias-risk-assessments-in-criminal-sentencing) and their [methodology writeup](https://www.propublica.org/article/how-we-analyzed-the-compas-recidivism-algorithm). The decile-score histograms are the visual hook. |
| **What the Model Actually Weighs** | "Race-blind" inputs don't make a model race-neutral — proxies (priors, ZIP code, prior arrests) carry the signal. Kills the naive "just don't feed it race" fix. |
| **Two Families of Definitions** | Group vs. individual is the first fork. **Individual fairness**: [Dwork et al., "Fairness Through Awareness" (ITCS 2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3913). Group fairness is auditable but coarse; individual fairness is principled but needs a similarity metric nobody agrees on. |
| **Flavors of Group Fairness** | Metrics table. Reference: [Fairness and Machine Learning Ch. 3](https://fairmlbook.org/classification.html). Each row is a *reasonable* sentence in English; they are not jointly satisfiable. |
| **The Impossibility Result** | [Kleinberg, Mullainathan, Raghavan, "Inherent Trade-Offs in the Fair Determination of Risk Scores" (ITCS 2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05807) and [Chouldechova, "Fair prediction with disparate impact" (Big Data 2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00056). When base rates differ, no nontrivial classifier satisfies both equalized odds and equal PPV. Theorem, not implementation failure. |
| **So — Is COMPAS Unfair?** | ProPublica (FPR parity fails) and [Northpointe/Equivant response](https://www.equivant.com/response-to-propublica-demonstrating-accuracy-equity-and-predictive-parity/) (PPV parity holds) are both true. Impossibility theorem guarantees a tradeoff. Deeper lesson: the COMPAS debate was really a hidden debate over values. |
| **Open Questions** | **Feedback loops**: [Ensign et al., "Runaway Feedback Loops in Predictive Policing" (FAT* 2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.09847). **Strategic behavior**: [Hardt et al., "Strategic Classification" (ITCS 2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06980). Frontier: static fairness audits miss dynamics. Generative-AI fairness (ranking, personalization, LLM outputs) reopens every question at larger scale. |
| **Regulation Is Catching Up** | **EU AI Act high-risk obligations, effective August 2, 2026**: [Commission overview](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai); [Article 10 on data governance / bias examination](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/10/). Compliance is necessarily value-laden — the math says fairness can't be fully achieved, so "we couldn't define it" stops being a legal defense but doesn't dissolve the underlying choice. |
| **Takeaways** | Land: "fair" is not a certifiable property. The technologist's job is to make the tradeoffs legible to the people making the policy call. Tee up the [AI accountability debate `../../debates/accountability.md`](../../debates/accountability.md). |
