# Lecture 9 — Speaker Notes (Web Privacy and Tracking)

Per-slide context + clickable links. In-deck notes (the `::: {.notes}` blocks) are also visible via reveal.js speaker view (press **S**). Every URL was verified this session or is a canonical org landing page.

| Slide | Context + Links |
|---|---|
| **On the Internet, They Know You're a Dog** | The [1993 New Yorker cartoon by Peter Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Internet,_nobody_knows_you%27re_a_dog) is the trope — "On the Internet, nobody knows you're a dog." The whole lecture is an inversion joke. Ask the room: in the last hour of browsing, how many companies do you think saw at least one page you loaded? Honest answer is dozens. Companion hands-on: [Web Tracking activity](../../activities/web.md). |
| **What We'll Cover** | Map the arc: third parties + cookies → fingerprinting (harder problem) → linking → defenses. Classic exam framing to flag early: *"is deleting cookies enough?"* Answer: no (fingerprinting). |
| **What Is a "Third Party"?** | Recurrence is the whole game. One tracker embedded on N first-party sites sees you on all N. Named third parties students should recognize on a news site: Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, LiveRamp, Criteo, DoubleClick, Google Tag Manager. |
| **A Single Page, Dozens of Trackers** | Classic red-boxed screenshot: NYT homepage with every cross-domain request highlighted. The mechanism is **Cookie** (persistent ID) + **[Referer header](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Headers/Referer)** (which first-party page you were on). Fun aside: the HTTP spec misspells "referrer" as "referer" — baked in. |
| **Visualizing the Web of Trackers** | [Mozilla Lightbeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightbeam_(software)) was the original; the modern equivalent is the [Disconnect extension](https://disconnect.me/) visualization. Live-demo suggestion: browse for two minutes and show the graph — you always get a dense web with a handful of giant hubs (Google, Meta). |
| **Who Are the Third Parties?** | Consolidation is the punchline. Google trackers on ~75% of sites; Google Analytics on ~50% of page loads. Long tail: [LiveRamp](https://liveramp.com/), [Criteo](https://www.criteo.com/), [media.net](https://www.media.net/), Yandex. Numbers are from 2016–17 measurement work and are almost certainly higher now. |
| **Why They Track: Behavioral Targeting** | This is the business model — profiles feed **[real-time bidding (RTB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_bidding)** auctions in the milliseconds a page loads. Privacy harms worth naming: intellectual privacy (what you read), price discrimination, and **government piggybacking** on ad cookies — see the [Washington Post's 2013 story on NSA using Google PREF cookies to pinpoint surveillance targets](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/the-switch/wp/2013/12/10/nsa-uses-google-cookies-to-pinpoint-targets-for-hacking/). The **Belgian DPA's 2022 ruling** that the [IAB Europe TCF is unlawful](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=APD/GBA_(Belgium)_-_DOS-2019-01377) is the strongest anti-RTB precedent. |
| **Social Buttons Are Trackers Too** | Merely *loading* a Like button tells Facebook you visited. Vignette on the slide is the **Fashion ID** case: **[Fashion ID GmbH & Co.KG v. Verbraucherzentrale NRW eV, C-40/17 (CJEU, 29 Jul 2019)](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-40/17)** established site-embedder + Facebook as **joint controllers** for data the Like button collects. Follow-on: **[Meta Platforms Inc. v. Bundeskartellamt, C-252/21 (CJEU, 4 Jul 2023)](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-252/21)** knocked out most non-consent legal bases for combining off-platform data. Germany's Federal Cartel Office closed its Meta case in [October 2024 on that footing](https://www.bundeskartellamt.de/SharedDocs/Meldung/EN/Pressemitteilungen/2024/10_10_2024_Facebook.html). U.S. analog: the **[Meta Pixel healthcare wave](https://www.hipaajournal.com/meta-pixel-hipaa-violations/)** — dozens of hospital-website class actions and HHS/OCR guidance. |
| **First-Party Tracking with Third-Party Code** | No SOP boundary at all when the code runs as first-party JS. Session-replay + re-marketing scripts vacuum up form inputs — the fact pattern behind the wiretap and CIPA class-action wave. First parties must audit what they embed. |
| **How Cookies Enable Cross-Site Tracking** | Walk the four-step mechanic slowly (guaranteed midterm question). [`Set-Cookie` from server](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Headers/Set-Cookie) → automatic browser resend → third-party cookies sent across many first-party sites → Cookie + Referer links the ID to a specific page. |
| **Browsers Fought Back — Mostly** | Safari [ITP](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/) since 2020; Firefox [ETP / Total Cookie Protection](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2022/06/14/firefox-rolls-out-total-cookie-protection-by-default-to-all-users-worldwide/). **Vignette:** Google [reversed the third-party-cookie deprecation on April 22, 2025](https://privacysandbox.google.com/blog/privacy-sandbox-update) and [shut down most of Privacy Sandbox on Oct 17, 2025](https://privacysandbox.google.com/blog/privacy-sandbox-next-steps) (Topics, Protected Audience, Attribution Reporting, IP Protection, Related Website Sets). Roughly a year in, third-party cookies remain Chrome-default with **no timeline** — see [OneTrust summary](https://www.onetrust.com/blog/google-drops-plans-for-third-party-cookie-choice-prompt-in-chrome/) and [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/what-is-google-privacy-sandbox/). What survives: [CHIPS, FedCM, Private State Tokens](https://privacysandbox.google.com/cookies). |
| **"Just Delete Your Cookies"?** | The pivot. Sit with why deletion fails before revealing fingerprinting. |
| **Fingerprinting: Watching the Browser's Behavior** | Foundational paper: [Peter Eckersley, "How Unique Is Your Web Browser?", PETS 2010 (EFF)](https://coveryourtracks.eff.org/static/browser-uniqueness.pdf). No stored state to clear → nothing for the user to "delete." Entropy comes from the joint distribution. |
| **Canvas Fingerprinting** | Canonical measurement: [Acar et al., "The Web Never Forgets," ACM CCS 2014](https://securehomes.esat.kuleuven.be/~gacar/persistent/the_web_never_forgets.pdf). Fingerprinted by how your machine renders the letter "g." WebGL and AudioContext fingerprinting are the same idea applied elsewhere. |
| **Hands-On: Cover Your Tracks** | EFF's [Cover Your Tracks](https://coveryourtracks.eff.org/). Live-demo on the projector; have students report their results — always lands harder when it's their own browser. Companion assignment: [Web Tracking activity](../../activities/web.md). Complementary reference: [AmIUnique](https://amiunique.org/). |
| **"Anonymous" Tracking Isn't** | Two deanon paths: (1) log in to Google/Meta and the cookie ID is tied to a real identity across the whole web; (2) careless first-party programming leaks email/search terms via URL → Referer to every embedded third party. Search terms leak this way constantly. |
| **Cookie Syncing** | The glue of the tracking economy: independent databases reconcile IDs through the browser (the only thing both trackers can touch). Combine with **onboarding** ([LiveRamp](https://liveramp.com/) matching online IDs to offline retail/location data) and **cross-device tracking** and one tracker links across nearly every boundary. |
| **Referer and Cookie Blocking** | Referer blocking breaks the web (CSRF defense, cooperative tracking defeats it). Third-party cookie blocking is the pragmatic win that shipped by default in Safari (2020) / Firefox — but only half the problem, because fingerprinting needs no cookies. |
| **Request Blocking: The Most Effective Defense** | Tools: [uBlock Origin](https://ublockorigin.com/) · [EasyPrivacy filter list](https://easylist.to/) · [Disconnect](https://disconnect.me/) · [Ghostery](https://www.ghostery.com/). **The 2025–26 Chrome MV3 wrinkle:** Google permanently disabled Manifest V2 in [Chrome 138 (July 24, 2025)](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/migrate/mv2-deprecation-timeline); flag workarounds gone by [Chrome 151 (~July 2026)](https://thenextweb.com/news/chrome-manifest-v3-ublock-origin-content-blockers-disabled). The full uBO no longer runs on Chrome — users get [uBlock Origin Lite](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ublock-origin-lite/ddkjiahejlhfcafbddmgiahcphecmpfh) (declarativeNetRequest, static rule cap) or switch to Firefox/Brave. That the world's largest ad-network vendor also ships the world's largest browser is the conflict-of-interest teaching point. Companion breakout: [Web Privacy breakouts](../../breakouts/web-privacy.md). |
| **Opt-Out Signals: From "Do Not Track" to GPC** | Arc: [Do Not Track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do_Not_Track) was voluntary and universally ignored → [Global Privacy Control](https://globalprivacycontrol.org/) is legally backed and enforced. **Vignette:** [California AG's GPC page](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa/gpc); Sept 2025 CA/CO/CT joint sweep of businesses ignoring GPC ([Foster Garvey summary](https://www.foster.com/newsroom/legal-alerts/global-privacy-controls-preparing-for-the-next-wave-of-enforcement/)); [revised CCPA regs in force since Jan 1, 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/9/revised-and-new-ccpa-regulations-set-to-take-effect-on-jan-1-2026-summary-of-near-term-action-items) require confirmation that a GPC opt-out was processed. **12 states** now require UOOM recognition (CA, CO, CT, DE, MD, MN, MT, NE, NH, NJ, OR, TX — see [Didomi's 2026 GPC tracker](https://www.didomi.io/blog/global-privacy-control-gpc-2026)). California's **[AB 566 "Opt Me Out" Act](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB566)** (effective Jan 1, 2027) forces Chrome, Safari, and Edge to *ship* a GPC control. Landmark enforcement: **[Sephora $1.2M (Aug 2022, CA AG)](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-announces-settlement-sephora-part-ongoing-enforcement)** — first major GPC case; **Healthline $1.55M (July 2025)** — largest CCPA settlement to date, partly for failure to honor GPC. Bridge to the privacy-law lectures: technology proposes, regulation disposes. |
| **Takeaways** | Reconnect to the exam framing and the policy bridge. Deleting cookies is not enough (fingerprinting); purely technical opt-outs fail without legal backing — which is exactly why the next block of the course is privacy law. Debate for the term: [Privacy Omnibus / Web breakouts](../../breakouts/web-privacy.md) and the [IoT debate](../../debates/iot.md) both pull on this arc. |
