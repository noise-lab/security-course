| **Why Consumers Should Care** | The FTC's [2024 Social Media Report on advertising to children and teens](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/look-behind-screens-examining-data-practices-social-media-video-streaming-services) is the canonical inventory of harms (targeted ads, profiling, manipulation). Cambridge Analytica remains the go-to manipulation example. |
| **The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)** | Statute text: [Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1798.100 et seq.](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displayText.xhtml?division=3.&part=4.&lawCode=CIV&title=1.81.5). CPRA amended CCPA and created the [California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)](https://cppa.ca.gov/) — the first US privacy-only regulator. Dark-patterns ban text lives in the CCPA regulations at [11 CCR §§ 7004(a)–(c)](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/) — that's the "symmetry in choice / minimal steps / easy to understand" language the deck cites later. |
| **Who Is Subject to the CCPA?** | Note the 100,000-consumer threshold (CPRA raised it from 50,000). Thresholds 2 and 3 use *non-public* data — that's what makes automated coverage-detection so hard (see the Study 1 RQ4 slide). |
| **How You Are Supposed to Opt Out** | The [CCPA regulations §7014](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/) contain the exact wording, icon spec (blue toggle), and placement requirements. The [official "Your Privacy Choices" icon SVG](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/pdf/final_privacy_choices_icon.pdf). The statute's unusual prescriptiveness is what makes automated measurement feasible — the law literally hands us the keywords. |
| **Global Privacy Control (GPC): Opt Out Once** | Spec + adopter list: [globalprivacycontrol.org](https://globalprivacycontrol.org/). California AG's [Sept 2022 Sephora settlement press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-announces-settlement-sephora-part-ongoing-enforcement) was the first CCPA action to explicitly enforce GPC. [Colorado](https://coag.gov/office-sections/consumer-protection/consumer-credit-unit/colorado-privacy-act/) and [Connecticut](https://portal.ct.gov/AG/Sections/Privacy/The-Connecticut-Data-Privacy-Act) also now *require* honoring UOOM signals. |
| **A 2026 Enforcement Wake-Up Call** | Primary sources for the vignette: **Disney $2.75M (Feb 11, 2026)** — [CA AG press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/california-wont-let-it-go-attorney-general-bonta-announces-275-million); the AG found fragmented opt-out (each device / each streaming app opted-out separately), failure to honor GPC across services, and rejected Disney's defense that identity-based opt-out was "technically impossible" — quote: "if a business can associate a consumer's devices with the consumer for advertising purposes, it can and must associate those devices with the consumer for honoring opt-out rights." **General Motors $12.75M (May 8, 2026)** — [CA AG press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/when-it-comes-data-privacy-consumers-must-be-driver%E2%80%99s-seat-attorney-general); largest CCPA penalty to date; first CCPA action on **data minimization + purpose limitation** (CPRA additions, effective 2023); OnStar collected precise geolocation, hard-braking, seatbelt, and speed events and sold them to LexisNexis and Verisk for insurance rate-setting — $20M in unconsented data-broker sales 2020–2024; contrast with the separate Jan 2026 FTC-GM settlement that ended without a monetary penalty. **Healthline $1.55M (Jul 1, 2025)** — [CA AG press release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-announces-155-million-settlement-healthline) — previous record before Disney; same opt-out theme. **Sephora ($1.2M, 2022), DoorDash ($375K, 2024), Sling TV** are the earlier line — see the [CPPA and AG enforcement action tracker at IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/california-authorities-announce-largest-ccpa-fine-to-date). Cold-call prompts on the slide notes are still current. |
| **The Research Questions** | Study 1 paper: **Van Tran et al., "Measuring Compliance with the California Consumer Privacy Act Over Space and Time,"** *ACM CHI* 2024 — [ACM DL entry](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642597); [preprint / project page (Feamster group)](https://noise-lab.net/). RQ3 (spillover) is the policy-interesting one — connects directly to the "do we need a federal privacy law?" debate ([`../../debates/omnibus.md`](../../debates/omnibus.md)). |
| **Method: Vantage Points and Keywords** | The 5-state design (CA + VA/CO/UT + IL control) is the clever part — VA/CO/UT enacted privacy laws effective 2023 (natural pre/post experiment), IL has no comprehensive law (spillover control). [Illinois BIPA](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004&ChapAct=740%C2%A014/) is biometric-only, not comprehensive. The two-method detection (page-source + rendered/OCR) matters because dynamic-JS links may exist in only one. |
| **RQ1: Compliance Is Rising — but Incomplete** | Jan 2023 inflection = CPRA effective date. Ask: why did some sites migrate wording immediately and others take 6+ months? The Van Tran et al. paper's discussion of "silent-holdout" categories is worth naming (small e-commerce, regional media). |
| **RQ2: ~45% Fail the Appearance Requirements** | Numbers are for CA vantage point, Jul 2023, out of 428 sites with a link — Study 1 §5.2. Note: this counts *cosmetic* failures only; Study 2 shows the *functional* failure rate is worse. |
| **RQ3: Spillover to Other States** | 78% of sites showing the link in CA showed it in all five vantage points including IL — the "California effect" as [Anu Bradford, *The Brussels Effect*](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-brussels-effect-9780190088583) predicts. Policy relevance: a strong state law can set a de facto national floor. Caveat: a visible link in IL doesn't prove an IL resident can complete the opt-out — that's Study 2's question. |
| **RQ4: 30% of Covered Sites Have No Link** | "Likely subject to CCPA" is a proxy — thresholds 2 (100K CA consumers) and 3 (>50% revenue from data sale) use non-public data. Study 1 approximates with for-profit + revenue + HQ. Some sites claim "we don't sell" — that may satisfy pre-CPRA CCPA but not CPRA (which covers *sharing*). Willful evasion vs. honest ambiguity: often indistinguishable from outside. |
| **Beyond the Link: Does Opt-Out Actually Work?** | Study 2 paper: **Habib et al. / Van Tran et al. (compliance follow-up),** *IEEE S&P* or *USENIX Security* — [Habib et al., "'It's a scavenger hunt': Usability of Websites' Opt-Out and Data Deletion Choices," CHI 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3313831.3376511) is the foundational prior work; the deck's specific numbers come from the follow-up 1,000-site opt-out submission study. Dark patterns as a research construct: [Gray et al., "The Dark (Patterns) Side of UX Design," CHI 2018](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3173574.3174108) is the canonical taxonomy. FTC dark-patterns enforcement: [Amazon Prime "Iliad" settlement Sept 2024 (~$2.5B civil penalty + injunctive relief)](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-partial-summary-judgment-amazon-prime) — the paradigmatic modern dark-pattern case. |
| **Method: Actually Submitting Opt-Out Requests** | The methodological leap: don't just look — *do* the opt-out, with a unique synthetic identity per site, and track email confirmations for two months. Note the research-ethics dimension (mystery-shopper studies with synthetic identities) — Meeting 4's ethics thread returns here; typical IRB posture treats this as public-website interaction with no human subjects at risk. |
| **Opt-Out Control Methods** | The [OneTrust](https://www.onetrust.com/) irony deserves airing: sites pay a compliance vendor and are still out of compliance — misconfiguration, no functional testing, corner-case dark patterns baked into the vendor UI, or willful under-deployment. The EU analogue is IAB Europe's [Transparency and Consent Framework (TCF)](https://iabeurope.eu/transparency-consent-framework/), itself found unlawful by the [Belgian DPA and CJEU in 2024](https://gdprhub.eu/index.php?title=CJEU_-_C-604/22_-_IAB_Europe). |
| **~30% of Opt-Out Attempts Fail** | The failure taxonomy is the paper's own. The "ambiguous status" bucket (35 sites) is the most enforcement-interesting — a "Thank you" with no confirmation is what makes GPC-first regimes (opt out once, no per-site trust needed) so attractive. The identity-verification-abuse pattern (demanding driver's license or high-school test scores to opt *out*) is the canonical example of using compliance as a data-collection lever. |
| **Three Families of Dark Patterns** | Midterm material. Categories from [Brignull's Deceptive Patterns typology](https://www.deceptive.design/types) and Gray et al. above. The California regulations' operationalization at [11 CCR §7004](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/) is where these get statutory force: "symmetry in choice," "minimal steps," "easy to understand," "avoid manipulative design." |
| **Obstruction in the Wild: CAPTCHA to Opt Out** | Screenshot is from nfl.com's privacy portal (Study 2). Genuine ambiguity: [CAPTCHA has a real anti-bot rationale](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/bots/how-captchas-work/) but adds friction to a protected action. The CCPA regs are silent on CAPTCHA. This is the archetypal edge case that makes automated dark-pattern enforcement hard. |
| **Clear Violations vs. Loopholes** | The mapping table is the paper's own; use it to walk students from statutory hooks ("symmetry in choice" §7004(a)(2), "minimal steps" §7004(a)(3)) to the still-unaddressed patterns. **2026 update:** the Disney $2.75M settlement now squarely litigates fragmented per-device/per-service opt-out as a CCPA violation — one row from the "may violate" column just moved to "does violate." Watch for the first CPRA case explicitly citing §7004 dark-patterns language. |
| **The Enforcement Gap** | Bring the whole class together: automated measurement scales, human/legal judgment doesn't — but you need both. Study 1 finds the haystack; enforcement (Study 2 + the 2026 CA-AG actions) picks the needles. This is the natural break-out prompt at [`../../breakouts/compliance.md`](../../breakouts/compliance.md): should state AGs / CPPA fund continuous automated auditing? |
| **What Measurement Tells Policy** | Meta-point: empirical measurement holds privacy law accountable to its promises. The 2026 GM and Disney settlements are enforcement catching up to what these studies documented in 2023–24. Bridge back to Meeting 11's federal-vs-state debate ([`../../debates/omnibus.md`](../../debates/omnibus.md)) and forward to Meeting 13's AI + privacy content. |
