# Bonus — Speaker Notes (Net Neutrality)

Per-slide context + clickable links. Bonus deck (off-schedule). In-deck notes (`::: {.notes}`) are visible via reveal.js speaker view (press **S**). Terse — enough for a future instructor to pick up.

| Slide | Context + Links |
|---|---|
| **What "Net Neutrality" Actually Means** | Open by asking the room to define net neutrality in one sentence. Whole lecture unpacks why that sentence hides at least four distinct debates: censorship/discrimination, regulatory classification, interconnection economics, consumer protection. |
| **A 2025 Vignette: The Rules Are Off the Books** | **Verify before class.** ***Ohio Telecom Ass'n v. FCC***, Sixth Circuit, January 2, 2025 — vacated the FCC's 2024 Title II Order: [opinion PDF](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/25a0002p-06.pdf); coverage: [Ars Technica, "Net neutrality rules blocked by appeals court"](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2025/01/net-neutrality-rules-blocked-by-appeals-court/); [Reuters coverage](https://www.reuters.com/legal/us-appeals-court-strikes-down-fcc-net-neutrality-rules-2025-01-02/). ***Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*** (SCOTUS, June 2024): [opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-451_7m58.pdf) — ended Chevron deference and is the connective tissue with privacy/AI regulation across the course. As of 2026 no federal net-neutrality rules and no legislation. |
| **The Many Faces of Net Neutrality** | Portmanteau slide. Ask students *which* meaning they support/oppose. Bright-line rules poll well across party lines; Title II reclassification is far more contested. |
| **First, the Plumbing: The Internet Business Model** | Anchor: [BITAG report on Interconnection and Traffic Exchange](https://www.bitag.org/interconnection.php); [William Norton's *The Internet Peering Playbook*](http://drpeering.net/AskDrPeering/blog/articles/Peering_Playbook_files/The%20Internet%20Peering%20Playbook.pdf). Money flows opposite the "get-paid > free > pay" BGP-preference ordering. Neutrality debate lives at the *interconnection* layer as much as the last mile. |
| **The Bright-Line Rules (2015 Open Internet Order)** | [FCC 2015 Open Internet Order](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-releases-open-internet-order). Timeline: 2015 order → repealed 2017/2018 ([Restoring Internet Freedom Order](https://www.fcc.gov/restoring-internet-freedom)) → reinstated April 2024 ([Safeguarding and Securing the Open Internet](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-restores-net-neutrality)) → vacated January 2025. "Reasonable network management" is the escape hatch where the real arguments happen. |
| **The Legal Engine: Title II Reclassification** | Statutory hook: [Title II of the Communications Act, 47 U.S.C. §201 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/chapter-5/subchapter-II). Forbearance is what made the 2015 approach politically survivable — apply Title II *and* waive most of the utility-style regulation. Push students: if the goal is preventing discrimination, why exempt the CDNs that do the most traffic shaping? |
| **Congestion Wasn't "At Home" — It Was Upstream** | Empirical anchor: [Luckie et al., "Challenges in Inferring Internet Interconnection and Congestion" (USENIX Security 2014 / M-Lab 2014 report)](https://www.measurementlab.net/publications/interconnection-study/); [M-Lab's ISP Interconnection and Its Impact on Consumer Internet Performance (Oct 2014)](https://www.measurementlab.net/publications/isp-interconnection-impact/). Median Cogent→NYC-ISP throughput collapsed for months, recovered when paid-interconnection deals signed. |
| **Where Was the Bottleneck?** | Diagnosis matters legally: if the slowdown is at a paid interconnection border, it's an economic dispute about who provisions capacity — arguably outside bright-line rules targeting last-mile handling. |
| **So... Was Comcast "Blocking" Netflix?** | No clean villain. Cogent's own congestion management (prioritizing retail over wholesale during congestion) muddied the data. Good debate slide. |
| **Net Neutrality Around the World** | **India 2018:** [TRAI's Prohibition of Discriminatory Tariffs regulation (2016)](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/Regulation_Data_Service.pdf) blocked Free Basics; [DoT net-neutrality rules (2018)](https://dot.gov.in/relatedlinks/net-neutrality). **EU:** [Regulation 2015/2120 (Open Internet)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32015R2120), enforced by BEREC. Zero-rating / Free Basics is the international flashpoint. |
| **The Fight Moved to the States** | **California SB 822:** [text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=201720180SB822); ***ACA Connects v. Bonta***: [Ninth Circuit opinion (Jan 2022)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/01/28/21-15430.pdf); ISPs abandoned challenge May 2022. California law effectively sets a national floor because no one builds California-only network policy. |
| **Net Neutrality: Summary** | Three-part framing: classification (legal hook), bright-line rules (popular core), interconnection (fuzzy economics). Exam-worthy point: *why* the rules keep flipping — it's an administrative-law and statutory-authority problem, not a technical one. |
| **Thought Question** | Cross-link: [Net-neutrality debate `../../debates/net-neutrality.md`](../../debates/net-neutrality.md). The state-law-as-floor answer is where most reasoned students land — but push them on the federalism costs (patchwork, preemption uncertainty). |
