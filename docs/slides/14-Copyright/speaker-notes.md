| **Why Copyright Is in This Course** | Copyright feels like an entertainment-industry topic; for us it is tech-policy. The through-line is one idea (**transformative use**) and one judge (**Alsup**, N.D. Cal.) who shows up in both the Oracle API case (2012) and the 2025 Bartz v. Anthropic AI case. Tell students the four-factor test is a near-certain midterm question. The Constitution's copyright clause: [U.S. Const. Art. I, §8, cl. 8](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92preface.html). |
| **A 2026 Vignette: The Anthropic Decision** | Load-bearing facts, all verified. **June 23, 2025 ruling** (Bartz v. Anthropic, N.D. Cal.): training on books "exceedingly transformative"/fair use, but downloading 7M+ pirated books for a permanent library is not — [ArentFox Schiff analysis](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/ai-law-blog/bartz-v-anthropic-federal-judge-rules-ai-training-fair-use-but-piracy) · [Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/). **Sept 2025 settlement:** ~$1.5B, ~482,460 works, ~$3,000/work — [Authors Guild summary](https://authorsguild.org/advocacy/artificial-intelligence/what-authors-need-to-know-about-the-anthropic-settlement/) · [official settlement site](https://www.anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com/dates). **May 14, 2026 fairness hearing:** Judge Martínez-Olguín took under submission; claims rate **92.77%** (447,576 of 482,460 works); ~53 objections, minuscule opt-out rate; questions focused on attorneys' fees — [Publishing Perspectives](https://publishingperspectives.com/2026/05/anthropic-settlement-appears-to-cruise-through-its-final-fairness-hearing/) · [Authors Alliance takeaways](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/05/14/bartz-v-anthropic-fairness-hearing-observations-and-takeaways/) · [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/copyright/article/100438-little-drama-at-anthropic-s-settlement-hearing.html). Initial payments estimated **Aug 2026**. Anthropic funds in four installments (Oct 2025, Apr 2026, Sept 2026, Sept 2027). Alsup irony: same judge (former programmer) who ruled APIs uncopyrightable in Oracle in 2012. Don't overstate — the settlement resolved *piracy* liability; the *training-is-fair-use* holding is district-court precedent, not SCOTUS law. |
| **What Copyright Protects** | Idea/expression dichotomy is the root of the entire API debate. Anchor case: **Feist v. Rural** (1991) — phone-book listings are facts, not copyrightable. Software as copyrightable expression since [Computer Software Copyright Act (1980)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Software_Copyright_Act). |
| **The Rights a Holder Controls** | The [six exclusive rights of §106](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#106): reproduction, derivative works, distribution, public performance, public display, digital audio transmission. Each is separately licensable — central to the entertainment business. Buying a film on iTunes licenses display-to-yourself only. Scale: record-company suits against ISPs cover millions of works; statutory damages ([$750–$150,000 per work](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html#504)) stack into billion-dollar verdicts. Duration is currently **life + 70** ([1998 CTEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_Term_Extension_Act), aka "Mickey Mouse Protection Act"). |
| **Fair Use: An Affirmative Defense** | Codified at [17 U.S.C. §107](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#107). Pre-empt common misconceptions: crediting ≠ permission; "I changed 10%" is not a rule; Creative Commons is a *license*, not a fair-use exception. Reference resources: [Stanford Fair Use](https://fairuse.stanford.edu/) · [U.S. Copyright Office Fair Use Index](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/). |
| **The Four-Factor Test** | Statute at [§107](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#107). Read the four-clause list aloud if time. Barton Beebe's empirical study ("An Empirical Study of U.S. Copyright Fair Use Opinions, 1978–2005," 156 U. Pa. L. Rev. 549) showed Factor 1 is the strongest predictor of outcome — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1092368). Per the agenda, this is the highest-probability midterm item — the professor "put real money" on a for/against fair-use case-study prompt. |
| **Factor 1: Purpose and Character** | Transformativeness came from **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music** (1994, 2 Live Crew "Pretty Woman" parody) — [510 U.S. 569](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/). Justice Souter: copyright's goal "is generally furthered by the creation of transformative works." Music intuition: replaying a song = not transformative; sampling into a new track = transformative. This is the factor AI cases live or die on. |
| **Factor 2: Nature of the Work** | This is the factor that helped Google in Oracle: declaring code as *functional*, close to an unprotectable system. **Feist v. Rural** again — facts aren't copyrightable at all. In AI training, plaintiffs argue novels sit at the strong-creative end, so factor 2 leans their way even when factor 1 favors the AI company. |
| **Factor 3: Amount and Substantiality** | Two questions: quantitative *and* qualitative. Anchor cases: **Perfect 10 v. Amazon** (9th Cir. 2007) — entire thumbnail images copied, still fair use because transformative — [law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/06-55405/06-55405-2007-12-03.html). **Harper & Row v. Nation Enterprises** (1985) — ~300 words from a 200k-word memoir, NOT fair use because the most newsworthy passage was the "heart" — [471 U.S. 539](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/471/539/). Principle: amount is judged *relative to the transformative purpose* — copy only what you need. |
| **Factor 4: Effect on the Market** | Anchor case: **Sony v. Universal City Studios** (1984, Betamax) — time-shifting is fair use; home-video market then exploded — [464 U.S. 417](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/464/417/). In AI litigation, market harm is the authors' strongest factor; **Judge Chhabria's "market dilution" theory in Kadrey v. Meta** (June 25, 2025) is the direction future plaintiffs are being pushed — LLMs can flood the market with substitutable content — [Skadden summary](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/07/fair-use-and-ai-training). |
| **Case Study Divider: Google v. Oracle** | Set the stakes: narrow dispute (~11,500 lines / 0.4% of the Java API), huge principle. Oracle's theory would let a company own a standard everyone must license. |
| **Google v. Oracle: The Dispute** | Case history: Java created by Sun (1995); Oracle bought Sun (2010) and sued Google (2010) over Android's (2007) reuse of Java API declarations. Damages sought ~$9B. Full opinion: [Google LLC v. Oracle America, 593 U.S. ___ (2021)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/18-956_d18f.pdf). Timeline and briefs: [SCOTUSblog case page](https://www.scotusblog.com/case-files/cases/google-llc-v-oracle-america-inc/). |
| **Declaring Code vs. Implementing Code** | Restaurant analogy for non-CS students: declaring code is the menu listing; implementing code is the kitchen recipe. Google reused the menu so Java developers wouldn't have to relearn, and wrote its own kitchen. Breyer leaned on exactly this intuition; the briefs invoked the QWERTY analogy. |
| **The Eleven-Year Journey** | The procedural ping-pong is the point — expert judges disagreed four times over 11 years. **Judge Alsup** (a programmer) sided with Google twice. Breyer sidestepped the copyrightability question and decided on fair use — so the case is precedent about *fair use of functional code*, not about whether APIs are copyrightable. [Ars Technica plain-language summary](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2021/04/google-wins-supreme-court-case-against-oracle-saves-billions/) · [EFF analysis](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2021/04/victory-fair-use-supreme-court-reverses-federal-circuit-oracle-v-google). |
| **How the Court Applied the Four Factors** | Walk each cell. Unifying thread: Factor 1 drives the rest — because the use was transformative, amount is read generously and Oracle's lost license fee isn't cognizable harm. **Alito/Thomas dissent** (pp. 53–63 of the opinion): this weakens copyright's incentive to create. Worth mentioning for balance. |
| **Section Divider: From Code to Content** | The law is case-by-case. The internet is 500 hours of video per minute. Transition to enforcement infrastructure. |
| **Content ID: Filtering by Fingerprint** | [YouTube's Content ID explainer](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370). Audio spectral hashing + video perceptual hashing; 100M+ reference files. Vast majority of YouTube copyright *actions* run through Content ID (private ordering), not the DMCA (statutory). Trade-off: it operates *above* the legal floor, so it sweeps in things the law would protect. |
| **The DMCA §512 Safe Harbor** | Statute: [17 U.S.C. §512](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html#512). Notice → removal → shield from liability → counter-notice → 10–14 day restoration if no lawsuit. Structural problem: takedown-first; claimant reviews their own dispute; three strikes can delete a channel. EFF's critique: [Copyright filters as "censorship machines"](https://www.eff.org/issues/intellectual-property/copyright-filters). Distinguish from **DMCA §1201** anti-circumvention (separate regime, security-research implications — see coverage-notes for expansion). |
| **When Filters Can't Read Context** | Automated matching sees a match, not a purpose. Classic pattern: NASA/government footage claimed by a broadcaster that aired it — [The Verge, Feb 2021](https://www.theverge.com/2021/2/22/22295938/nasa-perseverance-mars-landing-youtube-copyright). Public-domain classical music flagged via performance-rights vs. composition-rights confusion. [Tom Scott's video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Jwo5qc78QU) on why fair-use filters are impossible is a good optional assign. Deep point: fair use needs human judgment about purpose; matching has none. |
| **Activity: Run the Four-Factor Test** | Scenario: 15-min film-criticism video with 2-min clip + frame-by-frame analysis. Expected: (1) transformative criticism → favors fair use; (2) creative film but published → roughly neutral; (3) ~2 min of ~100 min, fair unless it's the "heart" → favors; (4) unlikely to substitute, may promote → favors. Likely fair use. Punchline: a filter would probably block it anyway. Hands-on: [`activities/copyright.md`](../../activities/copyright.md). Extended discussion: [`breakouts/copyright.md`](../../breakouts/copyright.md). |
| **Copyright Meets AI Training** | The Oracle question is now the AI training question. **Bartz v. Anthropic (June 23, 2025):** training fair use; pirated library not — [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/ai-law-blog/bartz-v-anthropic-federal-judge-rules-ai-training-fair-use-but-piracy). **Kadrey v. Meta (June 25, 2025):** Judge Chhabria found Meta's use fair on this record, but warned "in most cases the answer will likely be [that AI training is illegal]" and endorsed a **"market dilution"** theory — [Goodwin summary](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/alerts-practices-aiml-northern-district-of-california-judge-rules) · [Jackson Walker](https://www.jw.com/news/insights-kadrey-meta-bartz-anthropic-ai-copyright/). **NYT v. OpenAI:** still in discovery; summary-judgment briefing concluded April 2, 2026; no merits ruling on fair use yet — [AI Lawsuit Tracker](https://ailawsuittracker.com/cases/new-york-times-v-openai/). Debate seed: is AI training more like Google's API copy (functional, transformative) or Napster (verbatim reproduction, market substitution)? ## Additional context

**Common student questions (pre-empt):**

- *"Can I use copyrighted music in my YouTube video if I credit the artist?"* — No. Credit ≠ permission. Background music in personal videos is typically not fair use.
- *"What about Creative Commons?"* — CC is a licensing system, not a copyright exception. Always check the specific license terms.
- *"If I change 10% of the work, is it fair use?"* — No such rule. Fair use depends on the four factors, especially transformativeness.
- *"Why did Google win but Napster lost?"* — Napster: verbatim copying for the same purpose (listening). Google: new purpose (smartphone platform). Transformativeness matters.
- *"Can AI-generated content be copyrighted?"* — U.S. Copyright Office position: no, human authorship required. AI-as-tool (Photoshop-style) is OK; pure AI output is not.

**Cases to have ready if students ask:**

- **Authors Guild v. Google** (2d Cir. 2015) — Google Books scanning + snippets = highly transformative fair use — [law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/13-4829/13-4829-2015-10-16.html). Often cited by AI companies as precedent.
- **Kelly v. Arriba Soft** (9th Cir. 2003) — search-engine thumbnails as transformative fair use (precursor to Perfect 10).

**Debate topics (route to `../../debates/copyright.md`):**

- Should platforms be strictly liable for user uploads (kill §512 safe harbor), or is the current notice-and-takedown regime the best available compromise?
- Should there be a statutory penalty for false/abusive DMCA takedowns?
- Is AI training on copyrighted works more analogous to (a) Google Books scanning, (b) Napster verbatim distribution, or (c) something new? What legal rule best fits?
