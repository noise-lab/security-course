| 1 | **Title** | Copyright and Fair Use. |
| 2 | **Why Copyright Is in This Course** | Copyright feels like an entertainment-industry topic; for us it is tech-policy. The through-line is one idea (**transformative use**) and one judge (**Alsup**, N.D. Cal.) who shows up in both the Oracle API case (2012) and the 2025 Bartz v. Anthropic AI case. Tell students the four-factor test is a near-certain midterm question. The Constitution's copyright clause: [U.S. Const. Art. I, §8, cl. 8](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92preface.html). |
| 3 | **A 2026 Vignette: The Anthropic Decision** | **Figure:** capture of the Authors Alliance post announcing final approval, [Jul 21 2026](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/07/21/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-receives-final-approval/) (click to open).<br>• **Read first:** [Authors Guild, what authors need to know (updated Nov 16 2026)](https://authorsguild.org/advocacy/artificial-intelligence/what-authors-need-to-know-about-the-anthropic-settlement/).<br>• **June 23, 2025 ruling** (Bartz v. Anthropic, N.D. Cal.): training on books "exceedingly transformative"/fair use, but downloading 7M+ pirated books for a permanent library is not — [ArentFox Schiff analysis](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/ai-law-blog/bartz-v-anthropic-federal-judge-rules-ai-training-fair-use-but-piracy) · [Kluwer Copyright Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/the-bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-understanding-americas-largest-copyright-settlement/).<br>• **Sept 2025 settlement:** ~$1.5B, ~482,460 works, ~$3,000/work — [official settlement site](https://www.anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com/).<br>• **July 20, 2026 final approval:** Judge Martínez-Olguín entered final judgment, rejected objections that the price was too low, cut the fee request ($300M, then $187.5M) to about $101.5M (6.8% of the fund); over 91% of eligible authors claimed; several appeals of the final judgment were filed — [Authors Alliance](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2026/07/21/bartz-v-anthropic-settlement-receives-final-approval/) · [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/judge-approves-record-1-5-billion-settlement-involving-anthropic/) · [Writer Beware](https://writerbeware.blog/2026/07/23/anthropic-settlement-update-final-settlement-approved/).<br>• **Payments:** first payments expected the week of Nov 23, 2026 at about 70% of each claim, because Anthropic funds in four installments ($300M by Oct 2 2025; $300M within a week of final approval; $450M by Sept 25 2026; $450M by Sept 25 2027) — Authors Guild page above.<br>• Alsup irony: same judge (former programmer) who ruled APIs uncopyrightable in Oracle in 2012. Don't overstate — the settlement resolved *piracy* liability; the *training-is-fair-use* holding is district-court precedent, not SCOTUS law. |
| 4 | **What Copyright Protects** | **Figure:** Graphviz idea/expression diagram — three unprotected ideas (a sorting method, phone-book facts, a recipe's ingredients) each "written down as" a protected expression (an implementation, a directory with original selection and arrangement, the cookbook's prose). Idea/expression dichotomy is the root of the entire API debate. Anchor case: **Feist v. Rural** (1991) — phone-book listings are facts, not copyrightable. Software as copyrightable expression since [Computer Software Copyright Act (1980)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_Software_Copyright_Act). |
| 5 | **The Rights a Holder Controls** | The [six exclusive rights of §106](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#106): reproduction, derivative works, distribution, public performance, public display, digital audio transmission. Each is separately licensable — central to the entertainment business. Buying a film on iTunes licenses display-to-yourself only. Scale: record-company suits against ISPs cover millions of works; statutory damages ([$750–$150,000 per work](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap5.html#504)) stack into billion-dollar verdicts. Duration is currently **life + 70** ([1998 CTEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copyright_Term_Extension_Act), aka "Mickey Mouse Protection Act"). |
| 6 | **Fair Use: An Affirmative Defense** | **Figure:** capture of [17 U.S.C. §107 at Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/107) (click to open; the whole statute is one paragraph). Pre-empt common misconceptions: crediting ≠ permission; "I changed 10%" is not a rule; Creative Commons is a *license*, not a fair-use exception. Reference resources: [Stanford Fair Use](https://fairuse.stanford.edu/) · [U.S. Copyright Office Fair Use Index](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/). |
| 7 | **The Four-Factor Test** | Statute at [§107](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html#107). Read the four-clause list aloud if time. Barton Beebe's empirical study ("An Empirical Study of U.S. Copyright Fair Use Opinions, 1978–2005," 156 U. Pa. L. Rev. 549) showed Factor 1 is the strongest predictor of outcome — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1092368). Per the agenda, this is the highest-probability midterm item — the professor "put real money" on a for/against fair-use case-study prompt. |
| 8 | **Weighing the Four Factors** | **Figure:** Graphviz weighing diagram — four factor inputs feed one "weigh all four together" step with two outcomes (fair use / infringement); edge labels mark Factor 1 as the heaviest weight (Beebe) and Factor 4 as the plaintiffs' strongest ground in the AI cases. Make the point that the inputs are not scored and summed: a transformative purpose can carry whole-work copying (Google Books, thumbnails) and 300 words can sink a defendant (Harper & Row). Cold-call: keep "weigh together" or write bright lines? |
| 9 | **Factor 1: Purpose and Character** | Transformativeness came from **Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music** (1994, 2 Live Crew "Pretty Woman" parody) — [510 U.S. 569](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/510/569/). Justice Souter: copyright's goal "is generally furthered by the creation of transformative works." Music intuition: replaying a song = not transformative; sampling into a new track = transformative. This is the factor AI cases live or die on. |
| 10 | **Factor 2: Nature of the Work** | This is the factor that helped Google in Oracle: declaring code as *functional*, close to an unprotectable system. **Feist v. Rural** again — facts aren't copyrightable at all. In AI training, plaintiffs argue novels sit at the strong-creative end, so factor 2 leans their way even when factor 1 favors the AI company. |
| 11 | **Factor 3: Amount and Substantiality** | Two questions: quantitative *and* qualitative. Anchor cases: **Perfect 10 v. Amazon** (9th Cir. 2007) — entire thumbnail images copied, still fair use because transformative — [law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/06-55405/06-55405-2007-12-03.html). **Harper & Row v. Nation Enterprises** (1985) — ~300 words from a 200k-word memoir, NOT fair use because the most newsworthy passage was the "heart" — [471 U.S. 539](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/471/539/). Principle: amount is judged *relative to the transformative purpose* — copy only what you need. |
| 12 | **Factor 4: Effect on the Market** | Anchor case: **Sony v. Universal City Studios** (1984, Betamax) — time-shifting is fair use; home-video market then exploded — [464 U.S. 417](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/464/417/). In AI litigation, market harm is the authors' strongest factor; **Judge Chhabria's "market dilution" theory in Kadrey v. Meta** (June 25, 2025) is the direction future plaintiffs are being pushed — LLMs can flood the market with substitutable content — [Skadden summary](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/07/fair-use-and-ai-training). Forward pointer: the licensing-market slide (28) shows why this factor strengthens as paid deals accumulate. |
| 13 | **Case Study Divider: Google v. Oracle** | Set the stakes: narrow dispute (~11,500 lines / 0.4% of the Java API), huge principle. Oracle's theory would let a company own a standard everyone must license. |
| 14 | **Google v. Oracle: The Dispute** | **Figure:** capture of the opinion page at [Cornell LII, Google LLC v. Oracle America, No. 18-956](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/18-956) (click to open; syllabus, Breyer opinion, Thomas dissent). Case history: Java created by Sun (1995); Oracle bought Sun (2010) and sued Google (2010) over Android's (2007) reuse of Java API declarations. Damages sought ~$9B. Also: [supremecourt.gov slip opinion PDF](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/18-956_d18f.pdf) · [Oyez case page with oral argument](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2020/18-956) · [SCOTUSblog case page](https://www.scotusblog.com/case-files/cases/google-llc-v-oracle-america-inc/). |
| 15 | **Declaring Code vs. Implementing Code** | Restaurant analogy for non-CS students: declaring code is the menu listing; implementing code is the kitchen recipe. Google reused the menu so Java developers wouldn't have to relearn, and wrote its own kitchen. Breyer leaned on exactly this intuition; the briefs invoked the QWERTY analogy. |
| 16 | **The Eleven-Year Journey** | **Figure:** Graphviz strip under the table — five rulings alternating green (Google wins: 2012, 2016, 2021) and orange (Oracle wins: 2014, 2018). The procedural ping-pong is the point — expert judges disagreed four times over 11 years. **Judge Alsup** (a programmer) sided with Google twice. Breyer sidestepped the copyrightability question and decided on fair use — so the case is precedent about *fair use of functional code*, not about whether APIs are copyrightable. [Ars Technica plain-language summary](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2021/04/google-wins-supreme-court-case-against-oracle-saves-billions/) · [EFF analysis](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2021/04/victory-fair-use-supreme-court-reverses-federal-circuit-oracle-v-google). |
| 17 | **How the Court Applied the Four Factors** | Walk each cell. Unifying thread: Factor 1 drives the rest — because the use was transformative, amount is read generously and Oracle's lost license fee isn't cognizable harm. **Alito/Thomas dissent** (pp. 53–63 of the opinion): this weakens copyright's incentive to create. Worth mentioning for balance. |
| 18 | **Section Divider: From Code to Content** | The law is case-by-case. The internet is 500 hours of video per minute. Transition to enforcement infrastructure. |
| 19 | **Content ID: Filtering by Fingerprint** | **Figure:** Graphviz pipeline — reference files are fingerprinted into a 100M+ database; every upload is fingerprinted and compared; a match triggers the rights holder's preset policy (block / monetize / track), no match publishes normally. [YouTube's Content ID explainer](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370) (captured as `images/captures/contentid.png`, linked here rather than on the slide). Audio spectral hashing + video perceptual hashing. Vast majority of YouTube copyright *actions* run through Content ID (private ordering), not the DMCA (statutory). Trade-off: it operates *above* the legal floor, so it sweeps in things the law would protect. |
| 20 | **Content ID by the Numbers** | **Figure:** capture of [YouTube Copyright Transparency Report, summary page](https://transparencyreport.google.com/youtube-copyright/summary) showing the $100B+ and $12B+ headline tiles (click to open; the page is JS-rendered, so the year selector works only live).<br>• **Read first:** [TorrentFreak, Jun 6 2026, "YouTube processed 2.5 billion Content ID claims in 2025"](https://torrentfreak.com/youtube-processed-2-5-billion-content-id-copyright-claims-in-2025/) — the exact figures.<br>• 2025: 2,502,941,368 Content ID claims (+14% on 2024); over 99% of all copyright actions on YouTube; over 99% of claims automated; over 90% monetized; 12,840,608 disputes (0.51%); 67.42% of disputes resolved for the uploader; $12B+ cumulative ad revenue to rights holders by Dec 2024; 7,600+ partners; ~6% of webform removal requests judged likely false ownership claims.<br>• Point: the fair-use doctrine governs a tiny fraction of what happens; "monetize, not block" means Content ID is mostly a revenue pipe. |
| 21 | **The DMCA §512 Safe Harbor** | **Figure:** Graphviz notice-and-takedown flow — notice → expeditious removal (safe harbor) → counter-notice under penalty of perjury → 10–14 business days → suit (stays down) or restoration; dashed box for repeat-strike termination. Statute: [17 U.S.C. §512 at Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512) (captured as `images/captures/s512.png`; linked from the slide text). Structural problem: takedown-first; claimant reviews their own dispute; three strikes can delete a channel. EFF's critique: [Copyright filters as "censorship machines"](https://www.eff.org/issues/intellectual-property/copyright-filters). Distinguish from **DMCA §1201** anti-circumvention (separate regime, security-research implications — see coverage-notes for expansion). |
| 22 | **When Filters Can't Read Context** | Automated matching sees a match, not a purpose. Classic pattern: NASA/government footage claimed by a broadcaster that aired it — [The Verge, Feb 2021](https://www.theverge.com/2021/2/22/22295938/nasa-perseverance-mars-landing-youtube-copyright). Public-domain classical music flagged via performance-rights vs. composition-rights confusion. [Tom Scott's video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Jwo5qc78QU) on why fair-use filters are impossible is a good optional assign. Deep point: fair use needs human judgment about purpose; matching has none. |

| 25 | **The AI-Training Question as a Decision Flow** | **Figure:** Graphviz decision flow — (1) how were the copies obtained? pirated → Bartz: not fair use, $1.5B; lawful → (2) is training transformative? both N.D. | 26 | **Who Owns AI Output?** | **Figure:** capture of the [U.S. Copyright Office, Copyright and Artificial Intelligence page](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/) (click to open; reports and registration guidance).<br>• **Read first:** [Copyright and AI, Part 2: Copyrightability (PDF, Jan 29 2025)](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf) · [Library of Congress release](https://www.loc.gov/item/prn-25-010/copyright-office-releases-part-2-of-artificial-intelligence-report/2025-01-29/) · [Jones Day summary](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/02/copyrightability-of-ai-outputs-us-copyright-office-analyzes-human-authorship-requirement).<br>• **What it says:** human authorship required; AI as a tool does not bar protection; human selection/arrangement/modification of output is protectable for that part; prompts alone, however detailed or repeated, "convey unprotectable ideas"; no new legislation needed. Perlmutter:** Stephen Thaler sought to register "A Recent Entrance to Paradise" with his DABUS system as sole author and disclaimed human input; refused; district court and D.C. Circuit (2025) affirmed (the Act presupposes a human author: ownership, life-based duration, signatures); **cert denied March 2, 2026, No. 25-449** — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-final-word-supreme-court-refuses-to-hear-case-on-ai-authorship) · [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/ip-updates/supreme-court-declines-to-hear-thaler-v-perlmutter-leaving-human-authorship-requirement-intact.html) · [Baker Donelson](https://www.bakerdonelson.com/supreme-court-denies-certiorari-in-thaler-v-perlmutter-ai-cannot-be-an-author-under-the-copyright-act).<br>• Open question: *how much* human control suffices; Thaler disclaimed any, so no court has drawn the line. |

|  | **Additional context** | **Common student questions (pre-empt):**<br>• *"Can I use copyrighted music in my YouTube video if I credit the artist?"* — No. Credit ≠ permission. Background music in personal videos is typically not fair use.<br>• *"What about Creative Commons?"* — CC is a licensing system, not a copyright exception. Always check the specific license terms.<br>• *"If I change 10% of the work, is it fair use?"* — No such rule. Fair use depends on the four factors, especially transformativeness.<br>• *"Why did Google win but Napster lost?"* — Napster: verbatim copying for the same purpose (listening). Google: new purpose (smartphone platform). Transformativeness matters.<br>• *"Can AI-generated content be copyrighted?"* — Slides 26–27: human authorship required; AI-as-tool is fine; prompts alone are not; Thaler cert denied March 2026.<br>**Cases to have ready if students ask:**<br>• **Authors Guild v. Google** (2d Cir. 2015) — Google Books scanning + snippets = highly transformative fair use — [law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/13-4829/13-4829-2015-10-16.html). Often cited by AI companies as precedent.<br>• **Kelly v. Arriba Soft** (9th Cir. 2003) — search-engine thumbnails as transformative fair use (precursor to Perfect 10).<br>**Debate topics (route to `../../debates/copyright.md`):**<br>• Should platforms be strictly liable for user uploads (kill §512 safe harbor), or is the current notice-and-takedown regime the best available compromise?<br>• Should there be a statutory penalty for false/abusive DMCA takedowns?<br>• Is AI training on copyrighted works more analogous to (a) Google Books scanning, (b) Napster verbatim distribution, or (c) something new?
