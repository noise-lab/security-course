# Copyright and Intellectual Property

**Format.** Break into groups of 4–5. Each group picks **one** of the two breakouts below (or takes both if time allows). Spend ~5 minutes skimming the prep reads, then ~10 minutes debating. A designated reporter brings the group's position — and any dissents — back to the full class for a ~3-minute report-back.

Lecture 14 anchors on *Bartz v. Anthropic* (Judge Alsup, N.D. Cal., June 23, 2025): training on books was "exceedingly transformative" fair use, but downloading 7M+ pirated books to build a permanent library was *not* — leading to a reported ~$1.5B settlement. Same judge who ruled APIs uncopyrightable in Oracle. The unified thread: transformative use is winning, but *lawful acquisition* is a separate hurdle.

## Breakout A: The Four Factors in 2026 — Doctrine or Vibes?
<!-- breakout id="A" status="current" refreshed="2026-07-16" -->

**Motion.** *"The Bartz and Kadrey v. Meta rulings show that Factor 1 (transformative use) now overwhelms Factors 2, 3, and 4 in AI-training cases. If Factor 1 is the only factor that matters, we should stop pretending the four-factor test is a real doctrine and adopt a simpler rule: *any use that generates a materially new expression is fair use unless there is direct market substitution.*"*

<!-- current-events:start topic="fair-use-transformative-ai-training" -->
**Prep reads (5–10 min).**
- [Anthropic pays authors $1.5 billion to settle copyright infringement lawsuit](https://www.npr.org/2025/09/05/nx-s1-5529404/anthropic-settlement-authors-copyright-ai) — NPR, September 2025. The largest publicly reported U.S. copyright settlement: Judge Alsup found training "exceedingly transformative" but piracy sourcing not fair use; Anthropic pays ~$3,000 per work for ~500,000 works.
- [Kadrey v. Meta Platforms, Inc.](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/kadrey-v-meta-platforms-inc) — Loeb & Loeb, July 2025. Judge Chhabria's June 25, 2025 grant of partial summary judgment to Meta on fair use — but warning that plaintiffs "in most cases" with a better market-harm record will win.
- [Court shuts down AI fair use argument in Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/court-ai-fair-use-thomson-reuters-enterprise-gmbh-ross-intelligence/) — Reed Smith, 2025. The February 11, 2025 Delaware opinion — the counter-example: non-generative AI training on Westlaw headnotes lost on Factor 1 (not transformative) and Factor 4 (market substitution).
- [Two Federal Judges Rule In Separate Cases That Training AI Models Using Copyrighted Works is Fair Use](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/two-federal-judges-rule-in-separate-cases-that-training-ai-models-using-copyrighted-works-is-fair-use/) — Taft Law, 2025. Side-by-side analysis of Bartz and Kadrey, including the piracy-source split and why Meta escaped on the same facts Anthropic settled.
<!-- current-events:end -->

**Discussion prompts.**
- Barton Beebe's empirical study of fair-use cases: Factor 1 is by far the strongest predictor. Bartz, Kadrey/Meta, and Google v. Oracle all fit. Is the four-factor test *actually* a four-factor test, or has it always been Factor 1 with three follow-ons?
- Thomson Reuters v. Ross (Feb 2025) went the *other* way: training on Westlaw headnotes was NOT fair use, because Ross built a directly competing legal research tool. Is that Factor 4 (market harm) reasserting itself, or is it just that Ross's use was too "un-transformative"?
- The Anthropic $1.5B settlement — ~$3,000 per work times 500k class works — is the largest U.S. copyright settlement. It resolved the *piracy-source* claim, not the training claim. Are we now in a world where fair-use *use* is fine but lawful *acquisition* is the litigation target?
- The EU DSM Directive Art. 17 (upload filters) and the text-and-data-mining exception with a *rights-holder opt-out* create the opposite regime: transformative use is *not* automatically fair, but a specific mining exception is available if you don't opt out. Which model produces better outcomes for creators and for the AI industry?

**Bring back.** Your group's one-sentence description of how "transformative use" actually functions in 2026 doctrine, and one case (real or hypothetical) that would test it.

## Breakout B: Copyright Enforcement at Scale — Content ID and the Machinery
<!-- breakout id="B" status="current" refreshed="2026-07-16" -->

**Motion.** *"YouTube's Content ID (100M+ reference fingerprints; the vast majority of copyright actions on the platform are automated) has become de facto copyright *policy*: with statutory damages ranging from $750 to $150,000 per work, no reasonable creator would litigate a false claim. Congress should require that any automated copyright-enforcement system offering safe harbor to a platform must also offer symmetric penalties for bad-faith claims."*

<!-- current-events:start topic="content-id-automated-enforcement-dmca-1201" -->
**Prep reads (5–10 min).**
- [YouTube Processed 2.5 Billion Content ID Copyright Claims in 2025](https://torrentfreak.com/youtube-processed-2-5-billion-content-id-copyright-claims-in-2025/) — TorrentFreak, 2026. YouTube's transparency data: 2.5B Content ID claims in 2025 (up 14%), 90% monetized by rightsholders, and a 10x higher abuse rate on the webform than any other tool.
- [YouTube Copyright Transparency Report](https://transparencyreport.google.com/youtube-copyright/balanced-ecosystem) — Google, ongoing. Google's own dashboard: only ~4,454 of 7,626 approved rightsholders actively use Content ID; ~99% of claims are handled by the automated system with no human review.
- [Librarian of Congress Adopts New Exemptions Under Section 1201 of the DMCA](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/blogs/incontestable/librarian-of-congress-adopts-new-exemptions-under-section-1201-of-the-dmca.html) — Finnegan, 2024. The October 28, 2024 triennial exemptions — the "fail-safe" for fair use under §1201 anticircumvention — and how the process functions as a chokepoint more than a safety valve.
- [EFF to the FTC: DMCA Section 1201 Creates Anti-Competitive Regulatory Barriers](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2025/06/eff-files-comments-ftc-regarding-reducing-anti-competitive-regulatory-barriers) — Electronic Frontier Foundation, June 2025. EFF's argument that §1201 blocks fair-use circumvention by design and forces every noninfringing use through a burdensome administrative maze.
<!-- current-events:end -->

**Discussion prompts.**
- Content ID's outputs include monetization redirect (revenue goes to claimant) and demonetization/removal. False positives sweep up government/public-domain footage, licensed content, and fair-use commentary. Is there a version of DMCA §512 that could deter this without inviting suit-heavy chilling?
- DMCA §1201 (anti-circumvention) makes it illegal to break DRM even to exercise fair-use rights. Does §1201 survive a world where every use requires some degree of AI-mediated access to underlying works?
- The Bartz piracy question generalizes: is the correct default that AI training requires *lawful acquisition* (paid license, LibriVox-style open corpus, DPLA), or does that just centralize AI development in firms rich enough to license everything?
- Reverse engineering for interoperability (Sega v. Accolade) is protected fair use. Does the same reasoning cover training a *competing* AI on a *proprietary* model's outputs? Where does the line sit?

**Bring back.** The one automated-enforcement reform your group would legislate, and one type of claimant behavior your reform would fail to fix.

## Instructor notes

Breakout A is the doctrine version; Breakout B is the enforcement-machinery version. Both echo the whole-class [Copyright](../debates/copyright.md) debate but pick angles the debate doesn't cover. If time is short, run A — the "is fair use just one factor?" question is the freshest thing after Bartz/Kadrey and gets legally-minded students genuinely engaged. Both benefit from the four-factor test being posted on the board and students being pushed to score each rule under it.
