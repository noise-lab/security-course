# Privacy Law and Regulation

**Format.** Break into groups of 4–5. Each group picks **one** of the two breakouts below (or takes both if time allows). Spend ~5 minutes skimming the prep reads, then ~10 minutes debating. A designated reporter brings the group's position — and any dissents — back to the full class for a ~3-minute report-back.

Lecture 11's landscape as of 2026: ~20 comprehensive state privacy laws (no new one in 2025; Indiana/Kentucky/Rhode Island effective Jan 1, 2026); GDPR fines cumulatively ~€7B+, with TikTok €530M in May 2025 and the record Meta €1.2B still standing; FTC using Section 5 to reach entities HIPAA doesn't cover (GoodRx, BetterHelp); Disney COPPA $10M court-approved Dec 31, 2025. The wave is leveling and shifting to enforcement.

## Breakout A: Federal Omnibus — Finally, or Never?
<!-- breakout id="A" status="current" refreshed="2026-07-16" -->

**Motion.** *"The United States should adopt a federal omnibus privacy law modeled on GDPR — with a private right of action, meaningful preemption of state laws, and no small-business exemption. The 20-state patchwork has become the *de facto* national floor without any of the coherence of a real federal regime."*

<!-- current-events:start topic="federal-privacy-law-apra-preemption" -->
**Prep reads (5–10 min).**
- [US State Privacy Legislation Tracker](https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-privacy-legislation-tracker) — IAPP, ongoing. The canonical map: 19–20 comprehensive state laws in force as of January 1, 2026 (depending on whether Florida counts), with Indiana, Kentucky, and Rhode Island newly effective and no new law enacted in all of 2025.
- [New year, new rules: US state privacy requirements coming online as 2026 begins](https://iapp.org/news/a/new-year-new-rules-us-state-privacy-requirements-coming-online-as-2026-begins) — IAPP News, January 2026. Analyst-grade walkthrough of Jan 1, 2026 effective dates (Indiana/Kentucky/Rhode Island), the California ADMT / risk-assessment / cybersecurity-audit regs, and the Delete Act's opt-out platform launch — the patchwork the omnibus debate is now trying to preempt.
- [The American Privacy Rights Act | Congress.gov CRS report](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11161) — Congressional Research Service, 2024. Primary-source explainer of APRA's covered-data definitions, PRA, and preemption scope — the bill died in the 118th Congress in January 2025 and has not been reintroduced through mid-2026.
- [Irish Data Protection Commission fines TikTok €530 million and orders corrective measures following Inquiry into transfers of EEA User Data to China](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following) — Irish DPC, May 2025. What GDPR-with-teeth looks like in 2025: €530M penalty (with the Meta €1.2B still standing above it). The comparable US enforcement tool remains FTC Section 5 with orders-of-magnitude smaller penalties.
<!-- current-events:end -->

**Discussion prompts.**
- APRA / ADPPA and their predecessors have failed on two rocks: state preemption (California and others don't want to be preempted downward) and private right of action (industry lobbies against). Which is the more essential feature of a real federal law, and which is the more likely dealbreaker?
- Illinois BIPA has a private right of action and has generated massive settlements (Facebook $650M). GDPR has DPA enforcement but limited private action. Which model produces better outcomes — more litigation, or more regulator-driven enforcement?
- Compliance costs of the state patchwork fall hardest on mid-sized firms that can't afford a Chief Privacy Officer. Does a federal law with a small-business exemption defeat its own purpose, or is exemption necessary to make the law politically viable?
- GDPR's €530M TikTok fine (May 2025) and the €1.2B Meta record (2023) show real teeth. FTC's biggest privacy tool remains Section 5 "unfair or deceptive." Would a real federal law give the FTC statutory authority over data practices, or make Section 5 harder to use?

**Bring back.** Your group's one-sentence rule on whether the U.S. should adopt a federal omnibus, and the one provision your group could not agree on.

## Breakout B: FTC Section 5 Is Now the Privacy Regulator — Should It Be?
<!-- breakout id="B" status="current" refreshed="2026-07-16" -->

**Motion.** *"In the absence of federal privacy legislation, the FTC has stretched Section 5 'unfair or deceptive practices' to cover health apps (GoodRx, BetterHelp), location data brokers (Kochava, Mobilewalla, Gravy/Venntel), children's data (Disney COPPA), and 2FA-for-ads misuse (Twitter). This is regulation by consent decree, not law — and the FTC's Article-III limits and staffing make it structurally unfit for the job."*

<!-- current-events:start topic="ftc-section-5-privacy-enforcement" -->
**Prep reads (5–10 min).**
- [FTC Takes Action Against Gravy Analytics, Venntel for Unlawfully Selling Location Data Tracking Consumers to Sensitive Sites](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/12/ftc-takes-action-against-gravy-analytics-venntel-unlawfully-selling-location-data-tracking-consumers) — FTC, December 2024. Primary source for the ban (not just consent-gating) on sale of sensitive location data — 17B+ signals/day from ~1B devices, including Venntel data powering Babel Street's Locate X.
- [FTC Continues to Bring Enforcement Actions Against Data Brokers](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/blogs/wilmerhale-privacy-and-cybersecurity-law/20241216-ftc-continues-to-bring-enforcement-actions-against-data-brokers) — WilmerHale, December 2024. Lawyer's map of the Section 5 "unfair practice" theory — including the first-ever prohibition on collecting bidder-side data from real-time-bidding exchanges (Mobilewalla) and the Ferguson/Holyoak partial dissents that signal a Republican-led FTC may narrow the theory.
- [Court Approves Order Requiring Disney to Pay $10 Million to Settle FTC Allegations](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/court-approves-order-requiring-disney-pay-10-million-settle-ftc-allegations-firm-enabled-unlawful) — FTC, December 2025. COPPA-under-Section-5 in action: Disney's YouTube channel-level "Made-for-Kids" defaults triggered targeted-ad data collection on child viewers, generating a $10M civil penalty via DOJ referral.
- [Irish Data Protection Commission fines TikTok €530 million](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-tiktok-eu530-million-and-orders-corrective-measures-following) — Irish DPC, May 2025. Counterexample the motion invites: GDPR-scale enforcement under a purpose-built statute (Article 46 + Article 13) rather than a general-purpose consumer-protection statute — is that structurally cleaner or just differently limited?
<!-- current-events:end -->

**Discussion prompts.**
- The FTC's 2025 orders against Mobilewalla and Gravy/Venntel *banned* sale of sensitive location data (clinics, places of worship, LGBTQ+ venues) — 500M+ ad IDs sourced from real-time bidding. Would a federal privacy law have gone further, or would it have been narrower under industry lobbying?
- HIPAA doesn't cover health apps, wearables, or mental-health platforms. Section 5 does (BetterHelp $7.8M, GoodRx $1.5M). Is that "regulation by consent decree" a bug (unaccountable case-by-case) or a feature (adaptive to new harms)?
- Solove's taxonomy: aggregation, secondary use, exclusion, decisional interference. Section 5's "unfair" prong reaches conduct that causes "substantial injury not reasonably avoidable." Do those two frames converge, or does Section 5 stop short?
- The 2019 FTC/DOJ Facebook $5B penalty was framed as a Section 5 order-violation case, not a privacy-law violation. Is that a healthy use of prosecutorial creativity or a warning that we're stretching the tool past its limits?

**Bring back.** Whether your group thinks Section 5 is the right vehicle for privacy enforcement today, and one specific case (real or hypothetical) that would test your answer.

## Instructor notes

Breakout A is the whole-class debate in miniature. Breakout B is the more interesting one for students who care about administrative law and separation-of-powers — the "who *should* regulate" thread runs through the whole course. If time is short, run A. Both benefit from a running list on the board: for each recent enforcement action (Disney, GoodRx, Kochava, Meta, TikTok, GM), which statute made it possible? The distribution across FTC Act §5, COPPA, GDPR, CCPA, and state UDAP laws is the punchline.
